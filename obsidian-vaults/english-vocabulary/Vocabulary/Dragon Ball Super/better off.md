## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective phrase* (Most Common)
In a better situation, especially financially.

> [!example] Examples
> - We're better off now than we were five years ago.
> - Students from better-off families can afford to live alone.

### Sense 2 — *adjective phrase (advice)* (Common)
*Be better off (doing something / without something)* = it would be a better idea; it would be better for you.

> [!example] Examples
> - You'd be better off taking the train — it's faster.
> - You're better off without him.
> - We'd be better off starting early.

## 📜 Etymology
The comparative of *well off* (= in a good situation, 18th century).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Advice pattern: *You'd be better off + -ING*.
> - Opposite: *worse off*.
> - Compare [[well-off]] (= rich) — *better off* means in a better situation, not only about money.
> - ⚠️ Similar meaning in [[well-off]] (from [[Jujutsu Kaisen Modulo]])
