## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Common)
Small balls of ice that fall from the sky like rain.

> [!example] Examples
> - We got caught in a storm with hail.
> - Hail damaged the cars in the car park.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To call or wave to a taxi or person to make them stop; to greet.

> [!example] Examples
> - We hailed a taxi outside the station.
> - A friend hailed me from across the street.

### Sense 3 — *verb, "hail … as"* (Common)
To praise someone or something publicly and enthusiastically.

> [!example] Examples
> - The film was hailed as a masterpiece.
> - Engineers hailed the new battery as a breakthrough.

### Sense 4 — *phrasal verb, "hail from"* (Common)
To come from a particular place.

> [!example] Examples
> - Our professor hails from Ghent.
> - The band hails from Seattle.

### Sense 5 — *noun (figurative)* (Less Common)
A large number of things arriving at the same time: *a hail of bullets / criticism*.

> [!example] Examples
> - The hero ran through a hail of bullets.

## 📜 Etymology
Two different words: *hail* (ice) from Old English *hægl*; *hail* (greet, call) from Old Norse *heill* "healthy, whole" — the greeting *Hail!* meant "be healthy!" (related to *hale* and *wassail*).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - *Be hailed as* (passive) is very common in the news.
> - *Hail from* is a slightly formal or humorous way to say *come from*.
> - Weather vocabulary: *hail* (ice balls) vs *sleet* (rain + snow) vs *snow*.
