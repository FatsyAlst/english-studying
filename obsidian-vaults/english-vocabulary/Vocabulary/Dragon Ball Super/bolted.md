## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb (past of "bolt")* (Common)
Ran away suddenly, usually because of fear.

> [!example] Examples
> - The horse bolted when it heard the gunshot.
> - When the alarm went off, the thief bolted.
> - As soon as class ended, he bolted for the door.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
Locked a door or window with a bolt; fastened something with bolts.

> [!example] Examples
> - She bolted the door behind her.
> - The shelves are bolted to the wall.

### Sense 3 — *verb, "bolt (down)"* (Less Common — Informal)
Ate something very quickly.

> [!example] Examples
> - He bolted down his lunch and ran to the lab.

## 🔗 Collocations
- bolt for the door / exit
- bolt the door
- nuts and bolts (= the basic practical details)
- a bolt from the blue (= a complete surprise)
- sit bolt upright

---
## 📜 Etymology
Old English *bolt* "a short, heavy arrow (for a crossbow)"; *to bolt* = to dash away like an arrow (16th century). The lock and screw senses come from the arrow-like shape.

> - *A bolt from the blue* = a complete surprise.
