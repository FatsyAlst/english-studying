## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Common)
A false spoken statement intended to damage someone's reputation; the legal offence of making such statements.

> [!example] Examples
> - What he said about her is pure slander.
> - The politician sued the journalist for slander.
> - Don't believe that rumour — it's slander.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To say false things about someone in order to damage their reputation.

> [!example] Examples
> - He was accused of slandering his former boss.
> - They slandered her online for months.

## 📜 Etymology
From Anglo-French *esclaundre*, from Old French *escandle*, from Latin *scandalum* (Greek *skandalon* "trap, stumbling block") — so *slander* and *scandal* are the same word in origin.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Legal distinction: *slander* = spoken; *libel* = written or published. *Defamation* covers both.
> - In everyday speech it simply means saying false, harmful things about someone.
> - Portuguese: *calúnia / difamação* (not a cognate, but related to *escândalo*).
