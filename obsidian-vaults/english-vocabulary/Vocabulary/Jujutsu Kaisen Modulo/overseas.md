## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
In, to, or from a foreign country, especially one across the sea.

> [!example] Examples
> - She went overseas to study medicine.
> - The company has a large overseas market.
> - Overseas students often face higher tuition fees.

### Sense 2 — *adverb* (Most Common)
To or in a foreign country, especially one across the sea.

> [!example] Examples
> - He was sent overseas on a business trip.
> - Many young people dream of working overseas.
> - The troops were deployed overseas.

## 📜 Etymology
Compound of *over* + *seas*, originally meaning literally "beyond the seas." First recorded in the late 16th century. The term reflects Britain's island geography, where any foreign country could be reached by crossing the sea.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Functions as both adjective (*overseas markets*) and adverb (*travel overseas*) — no change in form.
> - More common in British English than American English, where *abroad* or *international* may be preferred.
> - Does not require an actual ocean crossing in modern usage — simply means "in another country."
> - No hyphen: it's *overseas*, not *over-seas*.
