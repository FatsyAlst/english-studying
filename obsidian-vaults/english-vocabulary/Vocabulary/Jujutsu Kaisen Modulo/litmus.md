## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A substance obtained from certain lichens that turns red in acidic conditions and blue in alkaline conditions, used as an indicator in chemistry. Most commonly known through the expression *litmus test*.

> [!example] Examples
> - The litmus paper turned red, indicating the solution was acidic.
> - Dip the litmus strip into the liquid and observe the colour change.
> - Litmus is one of the oldest and simplest acid-base indicators.

### Sense 2 — *noun — figurative* (Very Common)
A decisive test of someone's qualities, beliefs, or attitudes (used in the expression *litmus test*).

> [!example] Examples
> - The election became a litmus test of the government's popularity.
> - His response to the crisis was a litmus test of his leadership.
> - For many voters, this issue is the litmus test by which they judge candidates.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old Norse *litmosi*, literally "dye-moss," from *litr* ("colour, dye") + *mosi* ("moss"). Litmus is indeed derived from lichens (moss-like organisms). The figurative sense of *litmus test* as a decisive or critical test dates from the 1950s, borrowed from the chemistry usage where litmus definitively shows whether something is acid or alkaline.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - The figurative *litmus test* is far more common in everyday English than the chemistry meaning.
> - In American politics, "litmus test" often refers to a single issue used to judge a candidate's suitability (e.g., their stance on a key policy).
> - Related to [[gauge]] — both involve measurement and testing, but *litmus test* is more about a decisive pass/fail determination while *gauge* is about measuring degree or extent.
