## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adverb* (Most Common)
In an innate manner; as a natural, inborn quality rather than something learned or acquired.

> [!example] Examples
> - Some people seem to be innately good at mathematics.
> - Humans are innately social creatures.
> - She believed that talent was innately determined, not developed.

## 📜 Etymology
From *innate* + *-ly*. The adjective *innate* comes from Latin *innatus* ("inborn"), past participle of *innasci* ("to be born in"), from *in-* ("in, into") + *nasci* ("to be born"). Related to *natal*, *native*, *nation*, *nature* — all from the same Latin root for "birth."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Compare with [[inherently]] — very close in meaning, but *innately* emphasizes something present from **birth** (biological/genetic), while *inherently* emphasizes something that is an **essential, inseparable part** of a thing's nature (not necessarily biological).
> - Formal register — in everyday speech, *naturally* is more common.
> - The nature vs. nurture debate often centres on whether traits are *innately* determined or environmentally shaped.
