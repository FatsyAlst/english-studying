## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Common)
To decay or decompose; to undergo organic breakdown.

> [!example] Examples
> - The fruit began to rot on the counter.
> - If left untreated, the wound will rot.
> - The smell indicated something was rotting in the walls.

### Sense 2 — *Phrasal Verb: "rot away"* (Common)
To decay until something falls apart or disappears completely; to decompose entirely.

> [!example] Examples
> - The wooden pillars rotted away over decades.
> - The old rope had rotted away to almost nothing.
> - The building's foundation rotted away from water damage.

### Sense 3 — *Phrasal Verb: "rot away"* (Less Common)
To spend a long period of time in an unproductive or stagnant manner.

> [!example] Examples
> - He's been rotting away in that job for years.
> - Don't let yourself rot away doing nothing.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "rotian," related to Old Norse "rotinn" meaning "rotten." The Germanic root refers to decay or decomposition. The phrasal verb "rot away" developed with the adverb "away" to indicate complete decomposition.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - "Rot" can be used literally (physical decay) or figuratively (moral decay)
> - "Rot away" emphasizes complete decomposition or disappearance
> - The adverb "away" indicates the process continues until nothing remains
> - Used in both literal and figurative contexts
