## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Most Common)
To remove or eliminate something completely; to get rid of unwanted elements or people.

> [!example] Examples
> - We need to purge the database of old records.
> - The organization purged its files of sensitive documents.
> - The regime purged all opposition from the government.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To cleanse or rid of impurities; to make clean or pure.

> [!example] Examples
> - The ritual purged the temple of spiritual contamination.
> - He fasted to purge his body of toxins.
> - The cleaning solution purged the stains.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Common)
The process or act of purging; also refers to a sudden removal of many people, often political or violent.

> [!example] Examples
> - The file purge deleted gigabytes of data.
> - The political purge removed hundreds of officials.
> - The company conducted a purge of its staff.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin *purgare* (to cleanse), from *purus* (pure) + *agere* (to make). The prefix *pur-* relates to "pure" or "purify."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (eliminate): most common in modern usage, especially with data and organizational contexts
> - Sense 2 (cleanse): religious or medical contexts, often spiritual or health-focused
> - Can be used with abstract or concrete objects
> - "Purge" often implies thorough, complete removal, not just partial cleaning
> - Political/violent connotations can be associated when referring to removing people
> - Can be transitive (with an object) or intransitive (standalone action)
