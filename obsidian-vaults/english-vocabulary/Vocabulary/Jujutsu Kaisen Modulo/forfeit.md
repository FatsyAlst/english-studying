## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Common)
To lose or surrender something (especially a right, privilege, or possession) as a penalty or consequence; to give up something one is entitled to.

> [!example] Examples
> - If you break the rules, you forfeit your participation.
> - He forfeited his claim to the inheritance.
> - The team forfeited the match due to insufficient players.

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Common)
Something surrendered or lost as a penalty; a fine or penalty paid for breaking a rule.

> [!example] Examples
> - The forfeit was set at one month's wages.
> - In ancient times, the forfeit for treason was death.
> - They had to pay a forfeit for arriving late.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *forfait* (wrong, crime), derived from *forfaire* (to do wrong), composed of *for-* (beyond, wrongly) + *faire* (to do). The term originally referred to crimes that carried penalties.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal and official register
> - Often used in legal, sports, or organizational contexts
> - Verb: to lose something due to penalty or consequence
> - Noun: the thing lost or the penalty itself
> - Common in sports: "forfeit the game" (fail to show up)
> - Implies involuntary loss of rights or privileges
> - Stronger than "lose" because it implies rule-breaking
