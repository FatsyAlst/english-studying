## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Not strong or clear; slight; barely perceptible.

> [!example] Examples
> - There was a faint smell of perfume in the room.
> - She gave a faint smile and looked away.
> - I could hear a faint sound coming from the basement.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
Feeling weak, dizzy, and as if about to lose consciousness.

> [!example] Examples
> - She felt faint from the heat and lack of food.
> - He looked pale and faint after running the marathon.
> - The sight of blood made her feel faint.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To lose consciousness briefly; to pass out.

> [!example] Examples
> - She fainted when she heard the news.
> - Several people fainted during the long ceremony.
> - He fainted from exhaustion in the scorching sun.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *feint* ("sluggish, cowardly"), past participle of *feindre* ("to feign, pretend, be lazy"), from Latin *fingere* ("to shape, invent"). The semantic evolution went from "pretending to be weak" → "actually being weak" → "barely perceptible." By the 14th century, it had acquired its modern senses.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **"Not for the faint-hearted"** — idiom meaning something requires courage or nerve: *"This roller coaster is not for the faint-hearted."*
> - **"Haven't the faintest (idea)"** — idiom meaning to have absolutely no knowledge about something: *"I haven't the faintest idea where he went."*
> - Compare with [[dizzy]] — both describe physical sensations of weakness, but *dizzy* focuses on the spinning sensation while *faint* implies approaching loss of consciousness.
> - The verb *faint* and the verb *feign* share the same Latin root (*fingere*) — the original idea was pretending to be weak.
