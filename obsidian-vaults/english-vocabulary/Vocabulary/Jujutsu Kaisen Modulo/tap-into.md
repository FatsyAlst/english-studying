## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb* (Most Common)
To make use of or gain access to a source of energy, knowledge, money, or ability that already exists.

> [!example] Examples
> - She tapped into her inner strength to overcome the challenge.
> - Companies are eager to tap into new markets overseas.
> - The course helps students tap into their creative potential.

### Sense 2 — *phrasal verb* (Common)
To connect into or intercept something, especially a phone line or electronic communication.

> [!example] Examples
> - The police tapped into the suspect's phone conversations.
> - Hackers managed to tap into the company's database.
> - Intelligence agencies routinely tap into communications networks.

## 📜 Etymology
From *tap* (Old English *tæppa*, a stopper for a cask) combined with *into*. The metaphor is of opening a tap to access something that flows beneath the surface — like tapping into a water pipe or a keg. Extended figuratively to mean accessing any hidden resource or potential.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Inseparable phrasal verb — you cannot say *"tap something into"*; it is always *"tap into something."*
> - Highly versatile: works with abstract concepts (emotions, potential) and concrete ones (markets, databases).
> - Very common in business language: *"tap into new markets," "tap into demand."*
> - The surveillance meaning (wiretapping) comes from physically connecting to a telephone wire.
