## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Phrasal Verb* (Common)
To draw in or absorb something by suction; to pull or inhale something into a space.

> [!example] Examples
> - The vacuum sucks up all the dust from the carpet.
> - The drain sucks up water very quickly.
> - Make sure the filter doesn't suck up any debris.

### Sense 2 — *Phrasal Verb* (Common)
(Informal) To be agreeable, cooperative, or tolerant about something unpleasant; to accept and deal with a difficult situation.

> [!example] Examples
> - You just have to suck it up and accept the consequences.
> - He didn't like the decision but had to suck it up.
> - If you want the job, you'll have to suck up the long hours.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Informal)
A person who behaves obsequiously toward someone in power; a flatterer or yes-man.

> [!example] Examples
> - He's such a suck-up, always agreeing with the boss.
> - Don't be a suck-up; say what you really think.

---
## 📜 Etymology
"Suck" comes from Old English "sucan"; "up" is a particle intensifying the action. Used in various combinations since Middle English.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - "Suck it up" is a common idiom meaning to accept something difficult
> - "Suck up to someone" (different phrasing) means to flatter or be obsequious
> - The noun "suck-up" is derogatory and informal
> - Used frequently in casual, colloquial speech
