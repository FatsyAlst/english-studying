## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A sudden and great increase in the amount or number of something.

> [!example] Examples
> - There has been a surge in house prices recently.
> - We are having trouble keeping up with the recent surge in demand.
> - A last-minute surge in ticket sales saved the show from closure.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
A sudden increase of a strong feeling.

> [!example] Examples
> - She felt a sudden surge of anger.
> - He picked up the phone with a surge of panic.
> - A huge surge of emotion swept through her.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common)
A sudden, strong forward or upward movement; a powerful rush or flow.

> [!example] Examples
> - A surge of people poured through the gates.
> - A tidal surge caused severe flooding in coastal areas.

### Sense 4 — *noun, technical* (Common)
A sudden increase in the flow of electrical power through a system.

> [!example] Examples
> - An electrical surge damaged the computer's disk drive.
> - The National Grid was hit by a huge power surge.

### Sense 5 — *verb* (Most Common)
To rush, flood, or increase suddenly; to rise sharply.

> [!example] Examples
> - We all surged toward the door.
> - Share prices surged.
> - Relief surged through her.

---
## 📜 Etymology
Late 15th century, from Old French *sourgeon*; the verb partly from Old French stem *sourge-*, based on Latin *surgere* ("to rise"). Early senses included "rise and fall on the waves."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Always implies **suddenness and power** — temporary and dramatic, not gradual.
> - **Surge vs rise:** surge = sudden/powerful; rise = gradual/steady.
> - **Surge vs spike:** spike = sharper and briefer; surge = powerful but slightly longer.
> - "Surge protector" = device that protects electronics from power surges.
