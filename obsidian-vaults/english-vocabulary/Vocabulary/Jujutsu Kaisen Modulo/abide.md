## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Common)
To tolerate, endure, or bear something unpleasant; often used with "cannot" or "can't."

> [!example] Examples
> - I cannot abide dishonesty.
> - She couldn't abide the noise.
> - He doesn't abide fools.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Less Common)
To obey or comply with a rule, law, or agreement; to follow or adhere to.

> [!example] Examples
> - All members must abide by the club rules.
> - We agreed to abide by the contract terms.
> - They abide by traditional customs.

### Sense 3 — *Verb* (Archaic)
To remain or stay in a place; to dwell.

> [!example] Examples
> - Where shall I abide during my visit?
> - They abided in the valley for many years.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *abidan* (to wait, endure, remain), from *a-* (to) + *bidan* (to wait, bide). Related to the word "bide" (as in "bide one's time").

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 is modern and common, usually in negative constructions: "cannot/can't abide"
> - Sense 2 is formal and often appears in legal or institutional contexts
> - Sense 3 is archaic and rarely used in contemporary English
> - The adjective "abiding" is fairly common, meaning "lasting" or "enduring"
> - "Abide by" is distinct from "abide" (tolerate) — pay attention to the preposition
