## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A person or organization that owns a building or area of land and rents it to other people.

> [!example] Examples
> - The landlord increased the rent by 10% this year.
> - She reported the leaky roof to her landlord.
> - The landlord is responsible for maintaining the property.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Less Common — British)
A person who owns or manages a pub or inn.

> [!example] Examples
> - The landlord pulled a pint and slid it across the bar.
> - The local pub's landlord has been running the place for thirty years.
> - Ask the landlord if there are any rooms available.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English, compound of *land* + *lord* (from Old English *hlāford*, "loaf-ward" — the keeper of the bread, i.e., the master of the household). A landlord was literally the "lord of the land." The feminine form *landlady* follows the same pattern.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - The feminine form *landlady* is still used but is gradually being replaced by the gender-neutral *landlord* for all genders.
> - In British English, a *landlord* can refer specifically to the owner/manager of a pub — a distinctly British usage.
> - Compare with [[landlocked]] — shares the *land-* root but has a completely different meaning (surrounded by land with no coast).
> - A *slumlord* is a derogatory term for a landlord who neglects their properties, especially in low-income areas.
