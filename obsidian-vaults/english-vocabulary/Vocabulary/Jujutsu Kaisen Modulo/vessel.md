## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A large ship or boat.

> [!example] Examples
> - The coast guard rescued the crew of the stricken vessel.
> - A fishing vessel was spotted off the coast.
> - Enemy vessels were approaching the harbour.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
A hollow container, especially one used to hold liquid, such as a bowl, cup, or jug.

> [!example] Examples
> - She poured the wine into a crystal vessel.
> - Ancient clay vessels were discovered at the archaeological site.
> - The vessel was filled to the brim with oil.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common — Anatomy)
A tube-like structure in the body through which blood or other fluids flow.

> [!example] Examples
> - A blood vessel burst in his eye.
> - The arteries are the largest blood vessels in the body.
> - Damaged blood vessels can lead to serious medical conditions.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Less Common — Figurative/Literary)
A person regarded as embodying or containing a particular quality or serving as a medium for something.

> [!example] Examples
> - She became a vessel for the spirit of the ancient warrior.
> - The prophet was seen as a vessel of divine wisdom.
> - In the story, the child serves as a vessel for the cursed energy.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *vaissel* ("container, recipient, ship"), from Latin *vascellum*, diminutive of *vas* ("container, dish"). The dual meaning of "container" and "ship" has existed since Latin — a ship is metaphorically a large container that carries people and goods across water. The anatomical sense (blood vessel) dates from the 14th century, extending the "hollow tube/container" metaphor.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - In anime/manga contexts (especially Jujutsu Kaisen), *vessel* is used in Sense 4 — a person who serves as a container for a spirit, curse, or supernatural power.
> - As a word for "ship," *vessel* is more formal than *ship* or *boat* — common in maritime law, news reports, and official documents.
> - "Blood vessel" is the most common compound in everyday English.
> - The Latin root *vas* also gives us *vase* and *vascular* (relating to blood vessels).
