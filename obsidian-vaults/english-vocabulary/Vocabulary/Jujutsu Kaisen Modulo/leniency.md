## 📖 Definition
*noun* — Common

The quality of being lenient; mercy, clemency, or gentleness in judgment or punishment; the act of being less strict or severe than expected.

> [!example] Examples
> - The judge showed leniency toward the first-time offender.
> - The teacher's leniency with late submissions surprised everyone.
> - His parents' leniency allowed him too much freedom.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin *lenient-* 'soothing, softening,' from *lenire* 'to soften.' Entered English in 17th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal register; commonly used in legal and educational contexts.
> - Synonymous with clemency, mercy, and laxity (though laxity can be negative).
> - Related adjective: lenient (more commonly used than the noun).
> - Often indicates showing mercy or flexibility beyond strict rules.
> - Can be positive (compassion) or negative (excessive permissiveness), depending on context.
