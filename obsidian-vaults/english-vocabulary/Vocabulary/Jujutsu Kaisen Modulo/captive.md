## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Common)
A person or animal that is held as a prisoner or is confined.

> [!example] Examples
> - The captive was held in the tower for years.
> - The army freed the captives from enemy hands.
> - The zoo keeps animals, though they are technically captives.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Common)
Confined or imprisoned; unable to move freely.

> [!example] Examples
> - The captive audience had no choice but to listen.
> - The bird fluttered against the captive walls of its cage.
> - Captive markets often have higher prices.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French "captif," from Latin "captivus" meaning "captured, prisoner," from "capere" (to seize, capture).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - As a noun: refers to a person or animal that is imprisoned or confined.
> - As an adjective: describes a state of confinement or inability to leave.
> - Related to but distinct from "captivate" (to attract/charm) and "captivity" (state of being confined).
> - "Captive audience" is a common phrase meaning people who cannot leave or avoid listening.
