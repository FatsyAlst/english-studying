## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (Most Common)
Absolute; utter; used to emphasize the complete or extreme nature of something.

> [!example] Examples
> - It was sheer madness to attempt such a dangerous stunt.
> - The project succeeded through sheer determination.
> - The sheer number of people at the concert was overwhelming.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Common)
(Of fabric) Very thin and almost transparent.

> [!example] Examples
> - She wore a sheer white dress.
> - The sheer curtains allowed light to pass through.
> - Sheer silk stockings were fashionable in the 1950s.

### Sense 3 — *Adjective* (Common)
(Of a cliff, drop, or slope) Almost vertical; steep.

> [!example] Examples
> - The mountain had a sheer cliff face.
> - They looked down a sheer drop of 200 meters.
> - The sheer walls of the canyon were breathtaking.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English *schere*, probably from Old Norse *skærr* (pure, clear). The fabric sense likely developed from the transparent quality. The vertical/steep sense comes from the "clear" or "pure" directional meaning.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (absolute/utter) is the most emphatic and emphatic use
> - All three senses are common in contemporary English
> - When meaning "absolute," often used with abstract nouns
> - Can be used as an adverb emphasizing adjectives: "sheer chaos," "sheer brilliance"
