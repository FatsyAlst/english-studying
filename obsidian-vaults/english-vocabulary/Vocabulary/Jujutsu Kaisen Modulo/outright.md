## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adverb* (Common)
Completely and directly; in a way that is total and without reservation or qualification. Often used to emphasize the complete or absolute nature of something.

> [!example] Examples
> - He outright refused to cooperate.
> - I asked her outright if she was telling the truth.
> - The plan was outright rejected by the committee.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Common)
Absolute, complete, or utter; used to describe something that is total or unambiguous.

> [!example] Examples
> - That's an outright lie.
> - It was an outright disaster.
> - His outright hostility made everyone uncomfortable.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English "outrightly," meaning "straight out" or "directly." The term combines "out" (completely) + "right" (straight/direct), originally describing straightforward or honest dealings. Over time it came to mean absolute or complete.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can function as both an adverb and an adjective
> - As an adverb, emphasizes the complete or direct manner of action
> - As an adjective, describes something as absolute or total
> - Formal register; appropriate for professional and academic writing
> - Common in expressing strong negation or denial
