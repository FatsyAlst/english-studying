## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Common)
Small, hard, round balls made of glass, stone, or other materials, used in children's games.

> [!example] Examples
> - The children played with marbles in the schoolyard.
> - He collected rare marbles as a hobby.
> - Marbles rolled across the floor.

### Sense 2 — *Idiom: "lose one's marbles"* (Common)
To lose one's sanity, mental faculties, or ability to think clearly; to go crazy or act irrationally.

> [!example] Examples
> - If you keep talking like that, people will think you've lost your marbles.
> - He seems to have lost his marbles; he's acting very strangely.
> - Don't lose your marbles over this small problem.

### Sense 3 — *Idiom: "have all one's marbles"* (Common)
To be mentally sharp and alert; to be of sound mind and in control of one's faculties.

> [!example] Examples
> - Despite her age, she still has all her marbles.
> - You need to have all your marbles to solve this puzzle.

## 📜 Etymology
From French "marbre," from Latin "marmor," from Greek "marmaros" meaning "white or gleaming stone." The game using small balls called "marbles" developed as children's play. The idiom "lose one's marbles" likely developed from the metaphor of the mind as a container of marbles (thoughts/mental faculties).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Literal sense refers to the small glass or stone balls used in games
> - Idiomatic senses "lose one's marbles" and "have all one's marbles" are very common
> - "Lose one's marbles" is humorous or informal way of saying someone is acting insane
> - These idioms emphasize mental clarity or the lack thereof
