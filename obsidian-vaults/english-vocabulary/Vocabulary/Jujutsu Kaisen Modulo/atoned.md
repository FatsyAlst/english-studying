## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb / past participle* (Most Common)
Made amends for a wrongdoing; performed an act of reparation or compensation to show remorse for a past mistake, sin, or offence. Past tense of *atone*.

> [!example] Examples
> - He atoned for his crime by dedicating his life to helping others.
> - She atoned for her past sins by years of charitable work.
> - A crime that must be atoned for cannot simply be forgotten.

## 📜 Etymology
One of English's most transparent etymologies — *atone* is a contraction of the phrase **"at one."** The adverbial phrase *atonen* (c. 1300) meant "in accord, in harmony" — literally "at one." The verb emerged in the 1590s meaning "to be in harmony," shifting to "make reparations" by the 1680s. Remarkably, the noun *atonement* was coined first (c. 1513), and *atone* was later created by back-formation.

## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Always **"atone for"** — the verb requires *for* + noun/gerund. You cannot say *"atone something."*
> - Formal and literary; in informal contexts, *"make up for," "make amends,"* or *"apologize"* are preferred.
> - Carries strong religious and moral connotations — *atonement* is a central concept in Christian and Jewish theology.
> - **"Atone" vs. "repent":** *Repent* is about feeling remorse; *atone* is about taking action to repair harm.
> - **"Atone" vs. "make amends":** Both involve reparation, but *atone* carries more moral and spiritual gravity.
