## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adverb* (Most Common)
In a clear, direct, and straightforward manner; without ambiguity or unclear language.

> [!example] Examples
> - He spoke plainly about his concerns.
> - To put it plainly, you need to work harder.
> - She plainly stated her intentions.

### Sense 2 — *Adverb* (Common)
Without decorative elements, embellishment, or luxury; simply; in an unadorned manner.

> [!example] Examples
> - The room was plainly furnished.
> - She dresses plainly for work.
> - The building was plainly constructed.

### Sense 3 — *Adverb* (Less Common)
Obviously; clearly; in a way that is easily perceived or understood.

> [!example] Examples
> - It was plainly evident from his expression.
> - That plainly wasn't the answer we wanted.
> - The solution is plainly visible.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *plain* (flat, clear), from Latin *planus* (flat, even, clear). The adverb "plainly" developed from the adjective "plain" which has multiple related meanings.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (clear/direct): most common in contemporary usage
> - All three senses are fairly common in formal and informal English
> - Often used for emphasis: "to put it plainly" introduces a straightforward statement
> - Can modify verbs (plainly spoken), adjectives (plainly wrong), or stand alone
> - Slightly formal register, more deliberate than "simply" in many contexts
