## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adverb* (Most Common)
To or on one side; away from the main position or focus; set to the side.

> [!example] Examples
> - He pushed the chair aside to make room.
> - Set aside your worries for now.
> - She stepped aside to let him pass.

### Sense 2 — *Preposition* (Common)
Apart from; except for; with the exception of.

> [!example] Examples
> - Aside from the cost, it's a great idea.
> - Aside from a few errors, your work is excellent.
> - Everyone went, aside from John.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Common)
A remark made in an undertone or to the side, not meant to be heard by everyone; a theatrical technique where an actor speaks directly to the audience.

> [!example] Examples
> - "I know what he's really thinking," she said in an aside.
> - The character's aside revealed her true feelings.
> - He made an aside about the situation's irony.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *a side* (to the side), meaning "on/to the side." Developed from *a* (to) + *side*.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - As adverb: indicates physical or mental separation from the main focus
> - As preposition: "aside from" = "apart from" or "except for," introducing exceptions
> - As noun: common in literary and theatrical contexts; plural "asides" for multiple remarks
> - "Set aside" is a common phrasal concept meaning to reserve or allocate
> - In informal speech, "aside" often softens a comment: "Aside from that, how was it?"
