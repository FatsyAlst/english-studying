## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective / past participle* (Most Common)
Damaged, stained, or made impure; having one's reputation, honour, or purity tarnished or corrupted.

> [!example] Examples
> - His reputation was sullied by the scandal.
> - The once-pristine river was now sullied by industrial waste.
> - She refused to have her name sullied by false accusations.

### Sense 2 — *verb — sully* (Formal/Literary)
To damage or spoil something pure or clean, especially someone's reputation or character.

> [!example] Examples
> - He did not wish to sully his hands with gardening.
> - By cheating, they sullied the good name of their country.
> - The politician's legacy was sullied by corruption charges.

## 📜 Etymology
Probably from French *souiller* ("to soil, stain, make dirty"), from Old French *soillier*, ultimately from Latin *sucula* ("little pig") — via the idea of being muddied like a pig in dirt. The word entered English in the late 16th century and has always carried a formal, literary tone.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Very formal/literary — in everyday speech, *damage, ruin, tarnish, stain* are preferred.
> - The opposite, *unsullied*, is equally literary and means "spotlessly clean" or "untarnished": *"her unsullied reputation."*
> - Carries a strong moral connotation — something pure, noble, or clean has been corrupted or defiled.
> - Compare with [[shady]] — both relate to moral questionability, but *sullied* implies something *was* clean and has been *made* dirty, while *shady* implies inherent suspicion.
