## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To refuse to give something or to keep back something that is due to or desired by another person.

> [!example] Examples
> - She was accused of withholding evidence.
> - The witness was accused of withholding information.
> - The captain decided to withhold the terrible news even from his officers.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To suppress or hold back an emotion or reaction.

> [!example] Examples
> - She could not withhold her tears.
> - Her revenge is of a more passive-aggressive nature: she withholds her affections from him.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To deduct (tax) from wages before payment.

> [!example] Examples
> - She has $20 withheld from her paycheck every week.
> - The company withholds part of its employees' earnings for income taxes.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English *withholden*; first recorded 1150–1200. From prefix *with-* ("away, back") + *hold*, literally "to hold back."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **Irregular forms:** Past simple and past participle = **withheld** (NOT "withholded").
> - Transitive verb — always takes an object; often in passive: "information was withheld."
> - "Withhold judgment" = refrain from forming an opinion until more info is available.
> - "Name withheld" = identity kept private (common in journalism).
> - Antonym: give, grant, [[disclosed|disclose]], reveal.
