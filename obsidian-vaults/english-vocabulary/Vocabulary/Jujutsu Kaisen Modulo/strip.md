## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Common)
To remove or take off clothing or covering from someone or something.

> [!example] Examples
> - He stripped off his wet clothes after swimming.
> - The painter stripped the old wallpaper from the walls.
> - She stripped the bed and washed the sheets.

### Sense 2 — *Phrasal Verb: "strip away"* (Common)
To remove something that hides the true nature of something; to reveal something by removing layers, coverings, or pretense.

> [!example] Examples
> - The investigation stripped away all his lies.
> - When you strip away the fancy language, the core message is simple.
> - The accident stripped away his confidence.

### Sense 3 — *Phrasal Verb: "strip away"* (Common)
To gradually eliminate or reduce something important that has existed for a long time.

> [!example] Examples
> - Economic policies stripped away worker protections.
> - The new regulations stripped away many environmental safeguards.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "strieped," meaning to plunder or rob. Related to Old Germanic roots meaning "to turn" or "to twist." The phrasal verb "strip away" developed from the basic meaning of removal.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - "Strip" alone can mean to remove clothing or outer coverings
> - "Strip away" is more commonly used figuratively to mean removing something non-physical (lies, pretense, protections)
> - Used in both literal and metaphorical contexts
> - Common in writing and speech
