## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Most Common)
An accumulation of things stacked or piled together; a heap or mass of items.

> [!example] Examples
> - There was a pile-up of papers on her desk.
> - A pile-up of laundry waited to be washed.
> - The pile-up of unfinished work was overwhelming.

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Common)
A traffic collision involving multiple vehicles; a crash involving several cars.

> [!example] Examples
> - A major pile-up occurred on the highway.
> - There was a 10-car pile-up on the freeway.
> - The pile-up caused major traffic delays.

### Sense 3 — *Verb* (Phrasal)
To accumulate or gather in a heap; to stack up.

> [!example] Examples
> - Debts began to pile up.
> - Work piled up while he was away.
> - The snow piled up overnight.

## 📜 Etymology
From the verb phrase "pile up" (to stack or accumulate), combining "pile" (from Old French *pile*, a heap) and "up" (upward, accumulated).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (accumulation): general usage for any stacked items
> - Sense 2 (traffic accident): specific term used in news and traffic reports
> - Hyphenation varies: "pile-up" (hyphenated noun) vs. "pile up" (phrasal verb)
> - Sense 3 (verb): the phrasal verb form can be literal (physically stacking) or figurative (accumulating over time)
> - Very common in modern English for describing problems or items that accumulate
> - Traffic "pile-up" is standard terminology in news reporting
