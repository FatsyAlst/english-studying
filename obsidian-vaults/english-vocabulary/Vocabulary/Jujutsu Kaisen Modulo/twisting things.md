## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verbal phrase / idiom* (Most Common)
To distort or misrepresent facts, words, or situations, usually to manipulate someone or gain an advantage; to put a misleading spin on events.

> [!example] Examples
> - Stop twisting things — that's not what I said at all.
> - He's always twisting things to make himself look like the victim.
> - She accused him of twisting things around to avoid taking responsibility.

## 📜 Etymology
*Twist* comes from Old English *twistion* ("to twist, wring"), related to *twi-* ("two, double") — the root idea being two strands wound around each other. The figurative sense of distorting or manipulating arose naturally from the physical image of bending something out of its original shape.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Often used accusatively in arguments: *"You're twisting things!"* — implying dishonesty or manipulation.
> - "Twist someone's words" is the most common fixed expression for misquoting or distorting what someone said.
> - The adjective *twisted* can mean both physically contorted and morally perverse: *"a twisted mind"* or *"a twisted piece of metal."*
