## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (Most Common)
Deprived of the ability to feel or sense; lacking physical sensation or feeling.

> [!example] Examples
> - My fingers were numb from the cold.
> - The anesthetic made his arm numb.
> - His leg fell asleep and became numb.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Common)
Unable or unwilling to feel emotions; emotionally unresponsive or indifferent.

> [!example] Examples
> - She felt numb after hearing the tragic news.
> - He was numb with grief.
> - The constant pain left him emotionally numb.

### Sense 3 — *Verb* (Common)
To make numb; to cause to lose sensation or feeling.

> [!example] Examples
> - The cold numbed his fingers.
> - Fear numbed her ability to think clearly.
> - Shock numbed him to the pain.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English "nome," from Old Norse "numinn" (seized, taken). Originally related to the past participle of "nim" (to take). Semantic shift occurred over time.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can describe physical sensation or emotional state
> - Often used metaphorically for emotional responses
> - "Numbness" is the abstract noun form
> - "Numbingly" or "numbly" are adverbial forms
> - Often paired with descriptive phrases like "numb with shock"
> - Medical contexts use "numbness" to describe loss of sensation
