## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adverb* (Most Common)
Forward in space, direction, or movement; toward a destination; ahead.

> [!example] Examples
> - The army marched onward toward the city.
> - From that point onward, things improved.
> - She pushed onward despite the obstacles.

### Sense 2 — *Adverb* (Common)
Forward in time; from a certain point and continuing; into the future.

> [!example] Examples
> - From 2020 onward, the situation changed.
> - From that moment onward, he was changed.
> - Moving onward with the agenda.

### Sense 3 — *Adjective* (Less Common)
Moving or directed forward; advancing; progressing.

> [!example] Examples
> - The onward march of progress.
> - Onward movement is encouraged.
> - The onward journey continued.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *onweard* (toward, forward), from *on* (on) + *weard* (turned toward, -ward).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Both "onward" and "onwards" are acceptable; "onwards" is more common in British English
> - Sense 1 (spatial): used for physical movement or progress
> - Sense 2 (temporal): very common when discussing time progression; "from X onwards" is idiomatic
> - "And so onward" is an archaic or slightly formal way to indicate continuation
> - The adjective form is less common than adverbial uses
> - Can be used metaphorically for any progression, not just movement
