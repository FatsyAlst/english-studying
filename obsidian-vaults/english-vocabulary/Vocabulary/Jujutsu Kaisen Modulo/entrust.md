## 📖 Definition
*verb* (Formal)
To confer a trust on; to give someone a thing or a duty for which they are responsible. Implies confidence and trust that they will handle it properly.

> [!example] Examples
> - She was entrusted with the job of organizing the reception.
> - He didn't look like the sort of man you should entrust your luggage to.
> - Two senior officials have been entrusted with organizing the auction.

## 🔗 Collocations
- entrust someone with (a task / responsibility / a secret)
- entrust something to (someone / a bank / an advisor)
- be entrusted with (managing / organizing / leading)

---
## 📜 Etymology
Earliest known use in the Middle English period (1150–1500). From prefix *en-* ("to put into") + *trust* ("confidence"). Literally "to put into trust."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Two grammatical patterns: **entrust someone WITH something** (person-focused) or **entrust something TO someone** (object-focused).
> - **Common mistake:** Do NOT say "entrust someone to do something." Say "entrust someone with the task" instead.
> - Almost always positive connotation — implies trust, confidence, and respect.
