## 📖 Definition
*Adverb* — Common

Only, simply, or exclusively; nothing more than; used to emphasize that something is not as significant or extensive as it might appear.

> [!example] Examples
> - This is merely a beginning, not the final outcome.
> - He's not a villain, merely misunderstood.
> - The victory was merely temporary, not decisive.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From "mere" (only, simple), derived from Latin *merus* (pure, unmixed). The "-ly" suffix converts the adjective to an adverb.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal and objective tone
> - Used to downplay significance or limit scope
> - "Merely" emphasizes simplicity or limitation
> - Often appears in academic or literary writing
> - Can create a sense of dismissal or understatement
> - "Not merely...but also" creates contrast and emphasis
> - Synonyms: only, simply, just, merely, no more than
