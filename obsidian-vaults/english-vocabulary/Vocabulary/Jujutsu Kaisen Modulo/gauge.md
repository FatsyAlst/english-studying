## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To estimate or determine the magnitude, amount, or volume of something; to measure or assess.

> [!example] Examples
> - It was difficult to gauge his reaction from his expression.
> - Scientists use instruments to gauge atmospheric pressure.
> - The teacher tried to gauge the students' understanding of the topic.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
An instrument or device for measuring the magnitude, amount, or contents of something.

> [!example] Examples
> - The fuel gauge showed the tank was nearly empty.
> - A pressure gauge on the boiler indicated dangerously high levels.
> - Check the rain gauge to see how much precipitation fell overnight.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Less Common)
A standard measure of thickness, size, or capacity, especially of wire, sheet metal, or shotgun barrels.

> [!example] Examples
> - A 12-gauge shotgun is commonly used for hunting.
> - The wires were all different gauges.
> - Use a heavier gauge of steel for the support beams.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old Northern French *gauge* (modern French *jauge*), of uncertain origin, possibly from Frankish **galga* ("rod, pole") or a pre-Roman *galga*. Originally referred to a standard measure for casks of wine. The spelling *gauge* (rather than *gage*) became standard, though *gage* is an accepted American variant.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Spelling: *gauge* is standard; *gage* is an accepted American variant but far less common.
> - As a verb, most commonly used figuratively: *gauge someone's reaction, gauge public opinion*.
> - Related to [[litmus]] — both involve measurement, but *gauge* measures degree while a *litmus test* determines a binary yes/no.
> - The phrase "a gauge of" means "a way of measuring": *Customer satisfaction is the best gauge of quality.*
