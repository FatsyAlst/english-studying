## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To move in large numbers or a crowd, typically in a disorderly or agitated manner; to throng or congregate in great numbers.

> [!example] Examples
> - Bees swarmed around the hive.
> - Fans swarmed the streets after the game.
> - Insects swarmed over the food.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To be crowded or full of something; to teem with activity or presence.

> [!example] Examples
> - The beach was swarming with tourists.
> - The kitchen was swarming with ants.
> - The streets were swarming with protesters.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common)
A large number of things moving or gathered together; a dense crowd or cluster.

> [!example] Examples
> - A swarm of bees attacked the hive.
> - A swarm of locusts devastated the crops.
> - Swarms of fans arrived at the concert.

## 📜 Etymology
Old English *swearm* 'a crowd, multitude'; Proto-Germanic *swarmaz*. Originally used for bees, later extended to crowds of people.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Verb: describes movement in large, dense groups (often insects or crowds).
> - "Swarming with" = crowded with; full of.
> - Noun: a collective group (swarm of bees, swarm of locusts).
> - Often conveys a sense of overwhelming numbers or busyness.
> - Can suggest disorder, chaos, or aggressive behavior.
