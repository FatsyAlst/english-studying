## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To make something known publicly; to reveal information that was previously secret or hidden.

> [!example] Examples
> - The company has disclosed that it will be laying off thousands of workers.
> - The amounts of money were higher than previously disclosed.
> - The spokesman refused to disclose details of the takeover to the press.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Less Common)
To expose to view; to physically uncover something.

> [!example] Examples
> - The door swung open, disclosing a long dark passage.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English *disclosen*, from Old French *desclore*, from Vulgar Latin *disclaudere* — Latin *dis-* + *claudere* (to close, shut). First recorded 1350–1400. Literally "to un-close."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal register — very common in legal, business, financial, and official contexts.
> - Frequently used in passive voice: "It was disclosed that...", "Terms were not disclosed."
> - "Full disclosure" = complete revelation of all relevant information.
> - Antonym: [[withhold]] (refuse to reveal), conceal, suppress.
