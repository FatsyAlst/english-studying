## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A thing that makes someone less likely to do something; something that discourages or prevents action, typically because of feared consequences.

> [!example] Examples
> - Hopefully his punishment will act as a deterrent to others.
> - Some people say that capital punishment acts as a deterrent.
> - They've installed a security camera as a deterrent to thieves.

### Sense 2 — *noun, military* (Common)
A weapon or military capability maintained to prevent enemy attack, especially nuclear weapons.

> [!example] Examples
> - There has been a public call to rid the country of its nuclear deterrent.
> - The military maintains a strong deterrent against potential aggressors.

### Sense 3 — *adjective* (Less Common)
Serving to discourage, prevent, or inhibit.

> [!example] Examples
> - The ads had a deterrent effect on youth smoking.
> - Long deterrent sentences have undoubtedly acted as a warning to others.

## 📜 Etymology
Earliest known use 1820s (from Bentham, 1829). From Latin *deterrentem*, present participle of *deterrere* (to deter, frighten away) — *de-* ("from") + *terrere* ("to frighten"). *Terrere* is also the source of *terror*, *terrible*, and *terrific*.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - The root word *deter* is rooted in **fear** — a deterrent works by making people fear consequences.
> - **Deterrent** (noun) = the actual thing that deters; **deterrence** (noun) = the strategy or concept.
> - For a deterrent to work, it must be: known, perceived as real, significant, and likely.
> - Antonym: **incentive**, encouragement.
