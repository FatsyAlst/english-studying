## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
(formal) To pay careful attention to someone's advice, a warning, or a lesson, especially by acting accordingly.

> [!example] Examples
> - The airline was criticized for failing to heed warnings about safety.
> - If only they had heeded his warnings!
> - The government would do well to heed this advice.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common — Formal)
Careful attention or notice — used almost exclusively in fixed expressions: *take heed*, *pay heed*, *give heed*.

> [!example] Examples
> - Small businesses would be wise to take heed of the warnings.
> - I paid no heed at the time, but later I had cause to remember.
> - They gave little heed to the rumours.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *hēdan* ("to take care, attend, observe, protect"), related to Old Saxon *hōdian*, Dutch *hoeden* ("to guard"), German *hüten* ("to guard, watch"). All trace back to Proto-Indo-European **kadʰ-* ("to shelter, cover, protect"). The original sense of physical protection gradually shifted toward mental attention — guarding against danger by paying close attention.

## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal and literary — in everyday speech, *"listen to," "pay attention to," "follow"* are preferred.
> - As a verb, takes a direct object: *"heed the warning."* As a noun, survives only in fixed expressions: *take/pay/give heed*.
> - The adjective *heedless* (= paying no attention, reckless) is the most commonly used family member in modern English.
> - *Heedful* (= paying careful attention) exists but is rare and quite formal.
> - Typical objects: *warning, advice, call, lessons of history* — things one should listen to but often doesn't.
