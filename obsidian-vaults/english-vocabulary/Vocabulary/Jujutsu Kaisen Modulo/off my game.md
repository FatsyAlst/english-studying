## 📖 Definition
*Idiom/Expression* — Common

Not performing at one's usual level of ability, skill, or effectiveness; underperforming due to being distracted, tired, or out of practice. Feeling or appearing less sharp or competent than usual.

> [!example] Examples
> - I'm really off my game today; I can't seem to focus.
> - He played poorly last night; he was definitely off his game.
> - Sorry, I'm off my game—I didn't get much sleep.

## 📜 Etymology
The phrase "on one's game" refers to playing well or performing at one's best, likely stemming from sports terminology where "game" refers to one's competitive abilities and skill. "Off one's game" is the opposite, indicating subpar performance. This phrase became popular in sports culture and extended to general usage.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Commonly used in sports, gaming, and competitive contexts
> - Can apply to any performance or skill-based activity
> - Suggests temporary underperformance, not permanent inability
> - Often used to excuse or explain poor performance
> - Very common in informal, spoken English
