## 📖 Definition
*Noun* — Common

A member of a self-appointed group organized to prevent crime and punish offenders; a person who takes the law into their own hands by catching or punishing criminals without legal authority. Someone who administers justice outside the legal system.

> [!example] Examples
> - The vigilante tracked down the criminal himself.
> - Vigilante groups sometimes take justice into their own hands.
> - He acted as a vigilante, despite the danger.

## 📜 Etymology
From Spanish "vigilante," meaning "watchman" or "guard," derived from Latin "vigilans" (watchful), from "vigilare" (to watch). Entered English in the 19th century from Spanish, originally describing citizen groups that enforced law in frontier towns where official law enforcement was absent or ineffective.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Emphasizes taking justice outside official legal channels
> - Often carries connotations of both heroism and lawlessness
> - "Vigilante justice" is a common collocation suggesting unofficial punishment
> - Can be positive (protecting community) or negative (mob violence)
> - Used in legal, ethical, and narrative contexts
> - Modern vigilantism is often viewed critically in developed nations with established legal systems
