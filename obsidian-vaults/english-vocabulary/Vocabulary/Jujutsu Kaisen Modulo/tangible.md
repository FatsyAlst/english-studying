## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Real, concrete, and able to be touched or perceived by the senses; capable of being touched or felt physically.

> [!example] Examples
> - Tangible evidence was needed to convict him.
> - The benefits of exercise are tangible and measurable.
> - We need tangible results, not just promises.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
Able to be grasped mentally; clearly defined; real and specific rather than abstract or vague.

> [!example] Examples
> - The company showed tangible progress this quarter.
> - His fear was tangible and obvious to everyone.
> - There are tangible reasons for the decline in sales.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin *tangibilis* 'able to be touched,' from *tangere* 'to touch.' Entered English in 15th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal register; commonly used in business, legal, and academic contexts.
> - Often contrasted with intangible (non-physical, abstract).
> - Can mean both physically touchable and mentally/conceptually clear.
> - Opposite: intangible, abstract, vague.
> - Common in phrases like "tangible evidence" and "tangible results."
