## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Most Common)
To worry or be anxious about something; to feel concerned or troubled.

> [!example] Examples
> - Don't fret about the exam; you'll do fine.
> - She tends to fret over small details.
> - He fretted about the upcoming presentation all night.

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Common)
A state of worry or agitation; anxiety or irritation.

> [!example] Examples
> - The constant noise put him in a fret.
> - She was in a fret about the deadline.
> - Don't get into a fret over it.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Musical)
One of the narrow strips of wood, metal, or plastic on a guitar or similar stringed instrument that mark where fingers are placed.

> [!example] Examples
> - The guitarist's fingers slid along the frets.
> - A bent fret can affect the instrument's sound.
> - He pressed the string against the fret.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *fretan* (to devour, eat away), related to Old Norse *freta* (to chafe, gnaw). The sense "to worry" developed from the idea of being mentally eaten away by anxiety. The musical sense emerged in the 16th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (worry): informal and very common; similar to "worry" but slightly more literary
> - Often appears with "about" or "over" when specifying what is being worried about
> - "Don't fret" is a common reassurance phrase
> - Sense 2 (state of anxiety): less common in modern usage; "don't get in a fret" is somewhat dated
> - Sense 3 (musical): technical term, essential vocabulary for musicians
> - Adjective "fretful" means inclined to worry or easily agitated
