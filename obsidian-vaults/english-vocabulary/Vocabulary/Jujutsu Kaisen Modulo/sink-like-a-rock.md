## 📖 Definition
*phrase/idiom* — Common

To descend rapidly and heavily; to fail or decline very quickly and dramatically; used literally for sinking quickly in water or metaphorically for sudden failure.

> [!example] Examples
> - The stocks sank like a rock after the bad news.
> - If he steps off the pier, he'll sink like a rock—he can't swim.
> - The company's reputation sank like a rock.

## 📜 Etymology
Simile comparing the rapid sinking motion of a stone/rock in water to rapid decline or failure. "Rock" is older and more powerful than "stone" in this context. Phrase has been used since at least the 19th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Highly emphatic; suggests very rapid, dramatic descent or failure.
> - Literal use: something or someone without buoyancy will sink quickly.
> - Metaphorical use: projects fail, stocks decline, reputations collapse suddenly.
> - Opposite: "float," "stay afloat," "rise."
> - Similar idioms: "drop like a fly," "fall like dominoes," "go down in flames."
