## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Most Common)
To interlace threads, fibers, or strips by passing them alternately over and under each other; to create fabric or cloth.

> [!example] Examples
> - She weaves cloth on a traditional loom.
> - The threads weave together to create a pattern.
> - He learned to weave baskets from willow.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To move back and forth in a winding or twisting pattern; to navigate through obstacles by changing direction.

> [!example] Examples
> - The dancer wove between the other performers.
> - Traffic weaved through the city streets.
> - She weaved her way through the crowd.

### Sense 3 — *Verb* (Common)
To create or construct by combining different elements; to incorporate various parts into a coherent whole.

> [!example] Examples
> - The author weaves multiple storylines into the novel.
> - He weaves together fact and fiction.
> - The history weaves through the landscape.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "wefan", from Germanic roots meaning "to move back and forth." Related to "web."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Past tense can be "wove" or "weaved"; past participle is "woven" or "weaved"
> - "Woven" is more common in modern English for the past participle
> - Figurative uses are very common (weaving stories, weaving elements)
> - "Weaver" is a traditional occupation name
> - Related noun "web" comes from the same root
