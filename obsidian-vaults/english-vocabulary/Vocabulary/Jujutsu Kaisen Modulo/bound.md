## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To leap or jump, especially with a long springy step; to move with bounds.

> [!example] Examples
> - The deer bounded across the field.
> - He bounded up the stairs two at a time.
> - She bounded toward the door excitedly.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To move quickly in large jumps or leaps.

> [!example] Examples
> - The dog bounded through the grass.
> - The soldier bounded over obstacles.
> - Athletes bounded down the court.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To set limits on something; to confine or restrict within boundaries.

> [!example] Examples
> - The property is bounded by a fence.
> - Their friendship is bounded by mutual respect.
> - The area is bounded on all sides.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Common)
A limit or boundary; a restriction on extent or range.

> [!example] Examples
> - The bounds of propriety were exceeded.
> - Keep within bounds.
> - There are no bounds to their ambition.

### Sense 5 — *adjective* (Common)
Tied or fastened; restrained; obligated.

> [!example] Examples
> - The prisoner was bound with ropes.
> - She felt bound by duty.
> - He was bound for Paris.

## 📜 Etymology
Old English *byndan* 'to tie, bind'; Proto-Germanic *bundanan*. "Bound" as in jumping likely influenced by Old Norse *bonda*. "Boundary" related via Old French *bonde*.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - As a verb (jump): usually intransitive, describing vigorous movement.
> - As an adjective: "bound for" means heading toward; "bound by" means obligated.
> - Participle form: "The book is bound in leather" (fastened).
> - "Out of bounds" is common in sports and figurative contexts.
> - Past tense: bound (same as present for jumping sense).
