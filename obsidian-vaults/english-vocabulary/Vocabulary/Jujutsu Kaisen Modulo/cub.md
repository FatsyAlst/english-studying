## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A young animal, especially a young bear, lion, wolf, fox, or tiger.

> [!example] Examples
> - The mother bear fiercely protected her cubs.
> - Lion cubs learn to hunt by watching their parents.
> - The wolf cub stayed close to the pack.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common — Informal)
A young or inexperienced person, especially one who is new to an activity or organization.

> [!example] Examples
> - The veteran reporter took the young cub under his wing.
> - He was just a cub when he joined the team.
> - A cub reporter eager to prove herself on her first assignment.

## 📜 Etymology
Origin uncertain. First recorded in the 1520s, possibly from Old Irish *cuib* ("whelp") or Old Norse *kobbi* ("young seal"). Some etymologists link it to a Low German or Scandinavian source. The figurative sense of "inexperienced person" dates from the late 16th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Most commonly associated with bears, lions, and wolves — animals that are large predators.
> - "Cub reporter" is a well-known compound meaning a novice journalist — still used in newsrooms.
> - "Cub Scout" refers to a member of the youngest division of the Boy Scouts organization (ages 5–10).
> - Not used for young dogs (→ *puppy*) or young cats (→ *kitten*), even though they are also predators.
