## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (Most Common)
Not moving, shaking, or changing; firmly fixed or balanced; stable and secure.

> [!example] Examples
> - Hold the ladder steady while I climb.
> - The boat remained steady in the calm water.
> - His hands were steady and sure.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Most Common)
Continuous and even; not irregular or interrupted; proceeding at a constant rate.

> [!example] Examples
> - He maintained a steady pace throughout the race.
> - The steady rain continued all morning.
> - There was a steady stream of customers.

### Sense 3 — *Adjective* (Common)
Reliable and dependable; consistent in behavior or effort; trustworthy.

> [!example] Examples
> - She is a steady worker who never complains.
> - He has been a steady friend through everything.
> - The company showed steady growth.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "stede" (place, position), related to "stead" (in the capacity of). Extended to mean "standing firm" or "fixed in place."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can function as adjective, adverb, or verb depending on context
> - "Go steady" is an old-fashioned expression meaning to date someone exclusively
> - "Steady on" is a British exclamation meaning be careful or calm down
> - Often used in technical contexts to describe consistent measurements or rates
