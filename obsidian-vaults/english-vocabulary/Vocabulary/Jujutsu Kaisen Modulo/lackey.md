## 📖 Definition
*noun* — Common

A person who serves another in a subordinate capacity, especially obsequiously; a servant, footman, or someone who follows another person's orders without question; often used derogatorily.

> [!example] Examples
> - He dismissed his critics as mere lackeys of the regime.
> - The politician's lackeys followed his every command.
> - She refused to be a lackey in their scheme.

## 📜 Etymology
From French *laquais* (footman, servant); likely from Turkish *alaqu* or Arabic origin. Entered English in 16th century, originally meaning a footman or page, later acquiring derogatory connotations.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Strongly derogatory; suggests blind obedience and lack of independence.
> - Historical use: referred to a footman or servant in a household.
> - Modern use: implies someone is a puppet or tool of another.
> - Often used in political or social criticism.
> - Has negative connotations of servility and lack of integrity.
