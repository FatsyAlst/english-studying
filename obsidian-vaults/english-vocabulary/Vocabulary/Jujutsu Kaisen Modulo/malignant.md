## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective (Medical)* (Common)
Describing a cancerous tumor or condition that is dangerous and likely to spread to other parts of the body; capable of invading adjacent tissues and spreading distantly.

> [!example] Examples
> - The doctor diagnosed a malignant tumor.
> - Malignant cells are more dangerous than benign ones.
> - The cancer was confirmed as malignant.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective (Figurative)* (Less Common)
Evil, harmful, or having a dangerous influence; acting in a harmful way with intent to cause damage.

> [!example] Examples
> - His malignant influence corrupted the entire group.
> - The malignant gossip spread through the office.
> - A malignant force seemed to oppose their plans.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin "malignans," meaning "harmful" or "evil," derived from "malus" (bad) + "gignere" (to produce). The term entered medical usage in the 16th century when doctors began distinguishing between dangerous and non-dangerous tumors.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Primarily used in medical contexts to describe cancerous conditions
> - The opposite of "benign" in medical terminology
> - Key distinction: malignant tumors are cancerous and can spread; benign are not
> - Can be used figuratively to describe evil or harmful influences
> - Formal register; appropriate for medical and academic contexts
