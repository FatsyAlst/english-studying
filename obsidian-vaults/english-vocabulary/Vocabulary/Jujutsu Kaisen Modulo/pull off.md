## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb* (Most Common)
To carry out despite difficulties; to succeed in doing something difficult or unexpected.

> [!example] Examples
> - We pulled off the deal.
> - I never thought you'd pull it off.
> - The National League for Democracy pulled off a landslide victory.

### Sense 2 — *phrasal verb* (Common)
To remove something from a surface by pulling it.

> [!example] Examples
> - Pull off old blossoms so the plant will keep flowering.
> - As soon as she got home, she pulled off her clothes.

### Sense 3 — *phrasal verb* (Common)
(Of a vehicle) To stop by the side of the road; to turn off the main road.

> [!example] Examples
> - I pulled off the road at a small village pub.
> - After about a mile, we pulled off the main road onto a dirt track.

---
## 📜 Etymology
First recorded 1855–60. Combines "pull" (draw with force) + "off" (separation). The figurative meaning of "succeed at something difficult" developed from the literal sense of extracting something hard to move.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **Separable phrasal verb:** "pull off a victory" or "pull a victory off." Both positions work with pronouns: "pull it off."
> - Positive, celebratory tone when used for achievement — implies something was difficult or risky.
> - "Can they pull it off?" = common question when outcome is uncertain.
> - Transitive — requires a direct object.
