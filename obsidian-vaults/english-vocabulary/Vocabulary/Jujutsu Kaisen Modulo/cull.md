## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Most Common)
To select from a group or collection; to gather or collect items from among many options.

> [!example] Examples
> - She culled the best photographs from hundreds of options.
> - The editor culled stories from various sources for the anthology.
> - We culled the data to identify trends.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To reduce the population of an animal species by selective slaughter, typically to manage overpopulation or disease.

> [!example] Examples
> - Rangers culled the deer population to prevent starvation.
> - The government culled infected birds to stop the disease.
> - They culled the herd to maintain healthy numbers.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Less Common)
The items or animals selected for removal or collection; also refers to an animal judged unsuitable for breeding.

> [!example] Examples
> - The cull removed defective products from the batch.
> - A cull of the weakest animals improved the herd.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin *colligere* (to gather together), from *co-* (together) + *legere* (to choose, read). Old French *cuillir* developed this into the English "cull."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (select): academic and editorial contexts; choosing best items from many
> - Sense 2 (animal reduction): wildlife and agricultural contexts; can be ethically sensitive
> - Sense 1 is more general selection, while Sense 2 specifically involves removal/slaughter
> - Often appears in discussions of resource management and population control
> - In modern usage, "cull" sometimes carries negative connotations due to animal welfare concerns
> - Can apply to various contexts: culling data, culling applicants, culling products
