## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A valuable object that has belonged to a family for several generations, passed down from one generation to the next.

> [!example] Examples
> - This ring is a family heirloom passed down for four generations.
> - The old clock is one of our most precious heirlooms.
> - She wore an heirloom necklace at her wedding.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Less Common) — _formal/legal_
An item of personal property that, by law or custom, is inherited along with a family estate.

> [!example] Examples
> - The will listed several heirlooms to be distributed among the children.
> - Under old English law, certain heirlooms were tied to the family estate.

### Sense 3 — *adjective* (Common)
(Of a plant variety or animal breed) traditional, grown or raised for generations without modern commercial modification.

> [!example] Examples
> - We grow heirloom tomatoes because they taste better.
> - The farmer sells heirloom varieties of beans and corn.
> - Heirloom seeds help preserve agricultural biodiversity.

## 📜 Etymology
Compound of *heir* (from Old French *eir*, from Latin *heres* "heir") + *loom* (from Old English *gelōma* "tool, implement, utensil"). The "loom" in "heirloom" is unrelated to the weaving machine — it originally meant any tool or household object. Over time, the word narrowed to mean any valued possession passed down through a family.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **Heirloom** = a specific, treasured object passed down in a family; **inheritance** = all property and money left to heirs (legal term); **legacy** = broader, can be symbolic or abstract.
> - Very common in food/gardening contexts: "heirloom tomatoes" are traditional, open-pollinated varieties (as opposed to commercial hybrids).
> - Carries a strong emotional or sentimental connotation when referring to family objects.
> - The article used is "an heirloom" (the 'h' is pronounced, but both "a" and "an" are acceptable).
