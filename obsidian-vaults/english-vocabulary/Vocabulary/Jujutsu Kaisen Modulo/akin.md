## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Similar in nature, character, or quality to something; of the same type or related.

> [!example] Examples
> - What he felt was akin to despair.
> - The two languages are closely akin to each other.
> - Their approach to the problem was akin to throwing darts in the dark.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Less Common — Formal)
Related by blood; belonging to the same family.

> [!example] Examples
> - The two families were akin, sharing a common ancestor.
> - Many of the islanders are akin to one another.
> - They discovered they were akin through a distant cousin.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From *a-* (prefix, "of") + *kin* (Old English *cynn*, "family, race, kind"), from Proto-Germanic **kunją*, from Proto-Indo-European **ǵenh₁-* ("to give birth"). Literally means "of kin" — of the same family. The figurative sense ("similar to") developed from the idea that related things share family resemblance.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Always used predicatively (after a verb), never before a noun: *"It was akin to madness"* ✓, but not *"an akin feeling"* ✗.
> - Always followed by **to**: *"akin to"*, never used standalone.
> - Formal register — in casual speech, *similar to, like, close to* are preferred.
> - Compare with [[resemblance]] (Dragon Ball Z) — *resemblance* is a noun describing likeness, while *akin* is an adjective describing similarity.
