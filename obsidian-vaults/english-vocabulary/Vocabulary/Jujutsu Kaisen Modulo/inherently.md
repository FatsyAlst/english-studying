## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adverb* (Most Common)
In a permanent, essential, or fundamental way; as a basic and inseparable quality of something.

> [!example] Examples
> - There is nothing inherently wrong with the plan.
> - The system is inherently flawed.
> - Power is not inherently good or evil — it depends on how it's used.

## 📜 Etymology
From *inherent* + *-ly*. The adjective *inherent* comes from Latin *inhaerēns*, present participle of *inhaerēre* ("to stick in, cling to"), from *in-* ("in") + *haerēre* ("to stick, adhere"). Something *inherent* literally "sticks within" a thing — it's inseparable from its nature.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Compare with [[innately]] — very close in meaning. *Inherently* emphasizes something being an **essential, inseparable part** of a thing's nature. *Innately* emphasizes something present from **birth** (biological). A system can be *inherently* flawed (essential property), but a person is *innately* curious (born that way).
> - Formal register but widely used in academic, legal, and intellectual discourse.
> - Often used to make philosophical or analytical statements about the essential nature of things.
