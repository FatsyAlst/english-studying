## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Most Common)
To lose or cast off naturally; to drop or discard something that has been attached or covering.

> [!example] Examples
> - The snake sheds its skin annually.
> - Dogs shed fur in the spring.
> - Trees shed their leaves in autumn.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To allow something to fall off or away; to cast aside or get rid of something intentionally.

> [!example] Examples
> - She shed her old identity after moving.
> - He tried to shed his guilty conscience.
> - The company shed thousands of jobs.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Most Common)
A small, simple building or structure, typically used for storage or shelter.

> [!example] Examples
> - He kept his tools in the shed.
> - The old shed needed repairs.
> - They built a storage shed in the backyard.

## 📜 Etymology
The verb comes from Old English "sceadan" (to divide, separate). The noun "shed" is possibly from "sheding" (a shelter, hut), also from similar Germanic roots meaning "to shelter."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - As a verb: past tense is "shed" (not "shedded")
> - "Shed light on" means to illuminate or explain (figurative)
> - Shedding is a natural biological process for animals and plants
> - The noun "shed" is very common for garden/storage buildings
> - "Shed tears" is a poetic way of saying "to cry"
