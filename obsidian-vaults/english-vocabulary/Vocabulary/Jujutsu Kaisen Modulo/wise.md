## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (Common)
Having or showing good judgment, knowledge, or experience; able to make good decisions based on understanding.

> [!example] Examples
> - She gave me wise advice about career choices.
> - It was a wise decision to save money for emergencies.
> - The wise old man had seen many things in his lifetime.

### Sense 2 — *Phrasal Verb: "wise up"* (Common)
To become aware of the truth about something; to stop being foolish or naive; to recognize what is really happening.

> [!example] Examples
> - He finally wised up to their scheme.
> - You need to wise up about how the world really works.
> - She wised up to his lies and left him.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "wis," related to Old Germanic and Indo-European roots meaning "to see" or "to know." The original sense was "knowing" or "aware." The phrasal verb "wise up" developed in American English in the 20th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - "Wise" as an adjective means having good judgment and knowledge
> - "Wise up" is phrasal verb meaning to become aware or to stop being naive
> - "Be wise to" means to be aware of or suspicious of something
> - Both the adjective and phrasal verb are common in English
