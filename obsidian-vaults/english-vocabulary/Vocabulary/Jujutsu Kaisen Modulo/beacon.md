## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A bright light or signal used as a guide or warning, especially on ships at sea; a lighthouse or signaling light.

> [!example] Examples
> - The lighthouse beacon guided ships to safety.
> - The beacon flashed through the fog.
> - They followed the beacon to shore.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
A person or thing that gives hope, inspiration, or guidance; a symbol of hope or progress.

> [!example] Examples
> - Science is a beacon of hope for the future.
> - She was a beacon of strength during the crisis.
> - The movement became a beacon for change.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *beacen* 'sign, signal'; possibly related to *beacnan* 'to signal.' Proto-Germanic origin. Entered English in 6th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Literal sense: a light signal used for navigation or warning (maritime context).
> - Metaphorical sense: something that gives hope, guidance, or inspiration.
> - Often used in inspirational or formal contexts.
> - Common phrases: "beacon of hope," "beacon of light," "guiding beacon."
> - Formal register; more common in literary or formal writing.
