## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To make a long, narrow cut or opening in something, especially with a sharp instrument.

> [!example] Examples
> - He slit the envelope open with a knife.
> - She slit the fabric carefully along the dotted line.
> - The warrior slit his enemy's throat in one swift motion.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
A long, narrow cut, opening, or incision.

> [!example] Examples
> - Light came through a slit in the curtains.
> - She peered through a narrow slit in the wall.
> - The dress had a slit up one side.

## 📜 Etymology
Middle English *slitten*, from Old English *slītan* ("to tear, split"), related to Old Norse *slíta* and German *schleißen*. The past tense is the same as the base form: *slit* → *slit* → *slit* (irregular verb with no change).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Irregular verb: slit / slit / slit — the form never changes.
> - "Slit someone's throat" is a very violent and graphic expression; use with awareness of context.
> - As a noun, often describes narrow openings for light or visibility: *a slit in the blinds*.
> - "Slit" in fashion refers to a deliberate cut in a skirt or dress: *a thigh-high slit*.
