## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Common)
Mental sharpness and cleverness; the ability to think quickly and make intelligent connections; intelligence or common sense.

> [!example] Examples
> - He impressed everyone with his wit and humor.
> - Her quick wit made her a great comedian.
> - It took all his wits to escape the situation.

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Common)
Clever and amusing remarks or comments; the expression of humor through intelligent observation.

> [!example] Examples
> - His wit kept the dinner party entertained.
> - She's known for her sharp wit.
> - The speech was full of clever wit and wordplay.

### Sense 3 — *Idiom: "keep your wits about you"* (Common)
To remain calm and mentally alert in a difficult or dangerous situation; to stay in control of your mind.

> [!example] Examples
> - In an emergency, you must keep your wits about you.
> - She kept her wits about her during the accident.
> - If you keep your wits about you, you'll find a solution.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "wit," related to the verb "know" and meaning "knowledge" or "understanding." The sense of "mental sharpness" developed from the idea of cognitive ability. Related to German "Witz" (joke).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - "Wit" refers to intelligence expressed through clever humor or remarks
> - Often used in plural "wits" when referring to one's mental faculties
> - "Keep your wits about you" is a common idiom meaning to stay calm and alert
> - Can describe both the ability to make witty remarks and the remarks themselves
