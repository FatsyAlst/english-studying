## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To pull someone or something along the ground or floor with force, usually without lifting it; to haul.

> [!example] Examples
> - He dragged the suitcase across the floor.
> - The child dragged his feet as they walked.
> - She dragged him into the room.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To move or progress slowly or with difficulty; to become tedious or lengthy.

> [!example] Examples
> - The meeting dragged on for hours.
> - Time dragged while waiting for the results.
> - The story dragged in the middle.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To force someone to do something against their will; to compel someone to participate.

> [!example] Examples
> - She dragged him to the party.
> - I was dragged into the argument.
> - He dragged her along the path.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Common)
Something that hinders progress or is tedious; a burden.

> [!example] Examples
> - The paperwork is such a drag.
> - Dealing with traffic is a real drag.
> - These regulations are a major drag on growth.

## 📜 Etymology
Old Norse *draga* 'to draw, pull'; related to German *tragen* and Greek *tragein*. Entered English in 12th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - As verb: means to pull with force, often implying resistance.
> - "Drag on" = to continue tediously without ending.
> - "Drag out" = to extract reluctantly or prolong unnecessarily.
> - Noun: informal for something tedious or burdensome.
> - Physics: resistance force opposing motion through a medium.
