## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Most Common)
Courage, bravery, or determination; the mental strength or will to do something difficult or dangerous.

> [!example] Examples
> - It takes guts to admit you made a mistake.
> - She had the guts to stand up to the bullies.
> - He didn't have the guts to ask her out.

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Common)
The internal organs of the body; intestines or stomach area; also used as a plural noun for the digestive organs.

> [!example] Examples
> - He suffered a terrible gut wound.
> - The fish's guts were removed before cooking.
> - She felt butterflies in her guts.

### Sense 3 — *Adjective* (Less Common)
Instinctive or based on intuition rather than analysis; "gut feeling" or "gut reaction."

> [!example] Examples
> - My gut instinct told me not to trust him.
> - It's just a gut feeling, but I think something's wrong.
> - Trust your gut reaction.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *gut* and Old Norse *guttr*, originally meaning "channel" or "watercourse." The anatomical sense is the primary one, with the figurative "courage" sense developing later.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (courage): informal and colloquial; very common in everyday speech
> - Sense 2 (organs): can be singular or plural; "gut" is also used as singular
> - The verb "to gut" means to remove the internal organs, or figuratively, to destroy something completely
> - "Spill your guts" = to confess or reveal secrets
> - "Hate someone's guts" = to deeply dislike someone
> - "Gut-wrenching" describes something emotionally distressing
