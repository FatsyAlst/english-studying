## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adverb* (Most Common)
Utterly, completely, or thoroughly; used to intensify or emphasize an adjective or statement.

> [!example] Examples
> - That was downright dangerous.
> - The food was downright awful.
> - His behavior was downright rude.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
Complete or absolute; utter; not qualified or limited in any way.

> [!example] Examples
> - It was a downright lie.
> - That's a downright refusal.
> - A downright disaster.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English *down* + *right*; originally meant "directly downward" or "bluntly/frankly." Developed into intensifier meaning "thoroughly, utterly, absolutely."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Adverb: intensifies negative or strong adjectives (downright awful, dangerous, rude).
> - Adjective: emphasizes absoluteness (downright lie, refusal).
> - Slightly old-fashioned or formal tone; more common in British English.
> - Synonyms: absolutely, utterly, completely, entirely, thoroughly.
> - Usually used with negative or strong descriptors.
