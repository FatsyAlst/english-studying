## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb* (Most Common)
To appear or occur suddenly or unexpectedly.

> [!example] Examples
> - New coffee shops keep popping up all over the city.
> - A warning message popped up on my screen.
> - Problems kept popping up throughout the project.

### Sense 2 — *adjective/noun modifier* (Common)
Describing something temporary, makeshift, or appearing suddenly — used before a noun.

> [!example] Examples
> - There's a pop-up restaurant on the corner this weekend.
> - The store is running a pop-up sale in the park.
> - Pop-up ads are the most annoying thing on the internet.

## 📜 Etymology
From *pop* (Middle English *poppen*, imitative of a short, sharp sound or movement) + *up* (directional particle). The image is of something springing up suddenly, like a jack-in-the-box. The adjective/noun sense (*pop-up book*, *pop-up shop*) became widespread in the 21st century with temporary retail concepts and internet advertising.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - As a verb, it's two words: *"pop up."* As an adjective/noun before another word, it's hyphenated: *"pop-up shop."*
> - The verb is intransitive — things pop up; you don't pop something up (unless it's a tent, as in *"pop up a tent"*).
> - In computing, *pop-up* refers to windows or notifications that appear over the main content.
> - Very informal and conversational; suits casual writing and speech.
