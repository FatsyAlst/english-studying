## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *pronoun* (Archaic/Literary)
The objective (object) form of *thou* — an archaic/poetic word for "you" (singular), used when the person is the object of a verb or preposition, not the subject.

> [!example] Examples
> - Shall I compare thee to a summer's day? (Shakespeare)
> - We beseech thee, O Lord.
> - With this ring, I thee wed.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *thē*, the accusative and dative case of *thū* ("thou"). Traces back to Proto-Germanic **þek* and ultimately Proto-Indo-European **te-* ("thee, to thee") — one of the oldest pronouns in the Indo-European family. Cognates: Latin *te*, Spanish/Portuguese *te*, German *dich*, French *toi*. The decline of *thou/thee* began in the 15th century when the plural *you* started being used as a polite singular, eventually replacing *thou/thee* entirely in standard English by the 18th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **Subject vs. object:** *Thou* = subject ("thou art"), *thee* = object ("I love thee"). Mirrors the *I/me*, *he/him* distinction.
> - **Memory trick:** *Thee* rhymes with *me* → used like *me* (object). *Thou* rhymes with *now* → used like *I* (subject). *Thy* rhymes with *my* → possessive. *Thine* rhymes with *mine* → possessive pronoun.
> - Still used in: religious texts/prayer, traditional wedding vows, Quaker speech, Yorkshire dialect, poetry, and literature.
> - In Shakespeare's time, *thou/thee* was used **downward** (superior to inferior) or between intimates; *you* was the polite/formal form. Using *thou* to a social superior was rude.
