## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common — Botany)
The main body or stalk of a plant or shrub; the trunk-like central part from which branches, leaves, and flowers grow.

> [!example] Examples
> - The rose had a long, thorny stem.
> - Cut the stems at an angle to help them absorb water.
> - The plant's stem was thick and woody.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Very Common)
To originate in or be caused by something; to have a source or origin (followed by *from*).

> [!example] Examples
> - His problems stem from his childhood.
> - The conflict stems from a long history of mutual distrust.
> - Much of the confusion stems from poor communication.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To stop or restrict the flow or spread of something undesirable.

> [!example] Examples
> - They were unable to stem the tide of public opinion.
> - The government introduced measures to stem the flow of illegal drugs.
> - She pressed a cloth against the wound to stem the bleeding.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Common — Linguistics)
The root or base form of a word to which prefixes and suffixes are added.

> [!example] Examples
> - The stem of "running" is "run."
> - In linguistics, the stem is the part of the word that remains after removing inflections.
> - Irregular verbs often have different stems for different tenses.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *stemn*, *stefn* ("trunk of a tree, prow of a ship"), from Proto-Germanic **stamniz*. The verb sense "to originate from" (stem from) dates from the 1930s, while the verb sense "to stop" (stem the tide) comes from Old Norse *stemma* ("to dam up"). Despite identical spelling, these two verb senses have different etymological origins.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **"Stem from"** (originate) and **"stem"** (stop/restrict) are essentially two different verbs that happen to be spelled the same way.
> - "Stem cells" are so named because they are the "stem" (base) from which other specialized cells branch — like a plant stem spawning branches.
> - Compare with [[sprout]] (Dragon Ball Z) — both relate to plant growth; *stem* is the main structure, while *sprout* describes the act of new growth emerging.
> - "From stem to stern" — nautical idiom meaning "from one end to the other" (the stem is the front of a ship, the stern is the back).
