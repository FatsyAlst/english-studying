## 📖 Definition
*Idiom* — Common

To settle permanently in a place; to establish oneself and build a stable life in a community; to become established with the intention of staying long-term.

> [!example] Examples
> - After years of traveling, she finally put down roots in the city.
> - They moved to the village to put down roots and raise a family.
> - He was reluctant to put down roots in a place where he didn't feel at home.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From the metaphorical use of plant roots (which stabilize plants to a location) applied to human settlement. The phrase became common in the 19th-20th centuries.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Figurative expression, not literal digging
> - Suggests commitment to staying somewhere long-term
> - Often implies emotional or social attachment to place
> - Can indicate family-building or community involvement
> - Related to stability, permanence, and belonging
> - The opposite would be "uproot" or "leave"
> - Commonly used when discussing immigration or relocation
