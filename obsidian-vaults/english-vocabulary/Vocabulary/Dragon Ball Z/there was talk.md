## 📖 Definition
*Phrase* — Common

A phrase indicating that people were discussing or conversing about something; there existed rumors, discussion, or word-of-mouth communication about a topic.

> [!example] Examples
> - There was talk of a great tournament
> - There was talk among the villagers about his power
> - There was talk that he had died
> - There was talk of a legendary warrior arriving

---
## 📜 Etymology
Simple English construction combining "there was" (existential construction) + "talk" (conversation/discussion). "There was" is an impersonal construction indicating existence. "Talk" comes from Old English origins. The phrase is straightforward modern English combining these common elements.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Impersonal construction indicating discussion or rumors
> - Often used with "of" or "that" to introduce what was being discussed
> - Suggests second-hand information or rumors
> - Common in narrative contexts
> - More formal or literary than "people were saying" but similar meaning
> - Often used to set scene or provide background information
