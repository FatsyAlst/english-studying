## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb* (very frequent)
To tolerate or endure something unpleasant; to accept without complaint

> [!example] Examples
> - He won't put up with any disrespect
> - How long can they put up with this treatment?
> - I refuse to put up with such behavior
> - She has to put up with constant insults

### Sense 2 — *phrasal verb* (frequent)
To construct, erect, or build something

> [!example] Examples
> - They put up a barrier to block the path
> - The fighters put up defensive shields
> - He put up a tent for shelter
> - They put up walls to defend the fortress

### Sense 3 — *phrasal verb* (less frequent)
To lodge or accommodate someone; to provide lodging

> [!example] Examples
> - I'll put you up for the night
> - Can you put up my friend while he's visiting?

## 📜 Etymology
"Put" comes from Old English "potian" and "phrasal verbs" developed in English as a way to add nuance to basic verbs. "Put up" originally referred to literal construction, then extended metaphorically to mean enduring/tolerating (as in building up tolerance).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - "Put up with" is idiomatic; the two words work together to convey tolerance
> - In action contexts, often refers to mounting defense or creating barriers
> - The construction sense creates physical defensive structures
> - Casual and frequent in everyday English
