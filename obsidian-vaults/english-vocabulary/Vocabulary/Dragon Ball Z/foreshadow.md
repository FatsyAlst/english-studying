## 📖 Definition
*verb* (Common, Literary)

To indicate, suggest, or give a hint of something that will happen in the future; to be a sign or warning of something to come.

> [!example] Examples
> - The dark clouds foreshadowed the coming storm.
> - Early scenes in the film foreshadow the tragic ending.
> - His strange behavior foreshadowed the shocking revelation.

## 📜 Etymology
From "fore-" (before) + "shadow." The prefix "fore-" comes from Old English *fore* (before, in front). The metaphor uses "shadow" as something that appears before the actual thing itself, just as a shadow precedes a person moving toward light.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Literary/narrative technique term; common in literature and film analysis.
> - Related noun: "foreshadowing" (the technique itself).
> - Often used to analyze plot, narrative structure, and storytelling.
> - Implies that subtle clues point to future events.
> - Creates suspense or dramatic irony when recognized by the audience.
