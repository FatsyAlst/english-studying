## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (formal)
The human mind, soul, or spirit; the set of psychological characteristics and emotional patterns that make up an individual's personality and consciousness.

> [!example] Examples
> - Understanding Vegeta's psyche reveals the source of his prideful nature.
> - The intense training affected the warrior's psyche profoundly.
> - Goku's simple psyche contrasted sharply with the cunning minds of his rivals.

### Sense 2 — *verb/phrasal verb: "psyche out"* (informal)
To intimidate, unnerve, or discourage someone through psychological manipulation; to undermine someone's confidence or composure.

> [!example] Examples
> - Vegeta tried to psyche out Goku before their battle.
> - Don't let the opponent's threats psyche you out!
> - The crowd's jeers couldn't psyche out the confident fighter.

## 📜 Etymology
From Greek "psyche" (ψυχή), originally meaning "breath" or "soul," referring to the principle of life and consciousness. The word entered modern English via psychology and psychiatry. "Psyche out" is a modern slang verb derived from the noun, combining psychological tactics with "out" (to eliminate or undermine).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (noun) is formal and used in psychological or analytical contexts
> - Sense 2 (phrasal verb "psyche out") is informal and common in sports or competitive contexts
> - The phrasal verb implies temporary loss of confidence, not permanent damage
> - Can appear as "psyche out," "psych out," or simply "psych" in very informal speech
> - Related to the adjective "psychological" but distinct
