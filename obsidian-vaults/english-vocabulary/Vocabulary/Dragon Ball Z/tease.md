## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Common)
To make fun of someone in a playful or unkind way; to laugh at or mock someone; to provoke or make someone angry.

> [!example] Examples
> - The bullies teased him about his appearance.
> - She teased her brother playfully.
> - Don't tease the animal.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To tantalize; to suggest or promise something while withholding it; to tease someone's interest or desire.

> [!example] Examples
> - The trailer teases the upcoming movie.
> - He teased her with hints about the surprise.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Common)
A person who teases others; someone who makes fun of others.

> [!example] Examples
> - He's such a tease.
> - She's a good-natured tease.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *tæsan* (to pull, pluck, card wool), from Germanic root. The modern sense of "to make fun of" developed from the idea of pulling or provoking someone's reaction.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can be playful and friendly or mean and hurtful depending on context
> - Often implies repeated or prolonged action (not a one-time jab)
> - Synonyms (to make fun of): mock, ridicule, make fun of, poke fun at, jeer
> - Synonyms (to tantalize): tempt, dangle, withhold, provoke curiosity
> - Context determines whether teasing is affectionate or cruel
> - Common in interpersonal dynamics, especially among peers
