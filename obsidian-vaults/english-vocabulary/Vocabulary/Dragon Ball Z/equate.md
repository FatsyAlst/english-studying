## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (frequent)
To consider one thing as equal to or the same as another; to make equal; to establish a relationship of equality between things.

> [!example] Examples
> - You cannot equate raw power with true strength.
> - Don't equate their strength; one is far superior.
> - The two techniques should not be equated.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin "aequatus" (made equal), from "aequare" (to make equal), from "aequus" (equal). Entered English in the 16th century with both mathematical and metaphorical meanings.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Usually followed by "with" or "to"
> - Often used to show disagreement with comparisons
> - Formal and commonly used in logical or analytical contexts
