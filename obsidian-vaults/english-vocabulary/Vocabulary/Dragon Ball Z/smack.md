## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (common)
To hit someone or something hard, especially with the hand or a flat object; to slap forcefully.

> [!example] Examples
> - The fighter smacked his opponent across the face with tremendous force.
> - He smacked the ground, creating a massive crater.
> - Stop smacking each other and listen!

### Sense 2 — *noun* (common)
A hard blow or slap; the act of hitting something forcefully.

> [!example] Examples
> - The smack echoed across the arena.
> - He delivered a smack that sent the enemy flying backward.
> - The powerful smack rattled even the strongest warrior.

### Sense 3 — *adverb* (informal)
Directly or exactly; right in the middle (as in "smack in the middle").

> [!example] Examples
> - The attack landed smack on the target.
> - He jumped smack into the danger zone.
> - We hit the fortress smack center.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From Middle Dutch "smacken" or Low German "smacken," likely imitative in origin, representing the sound of a sharp blow. The word has Germanic roots and entered English around the 16th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (verb) is informal and common in action contexts
> - "Smack in the middle" is a popular idiom meaning directly in the center
> - Can also mean to suggest or have traces of something: "that smacks of dishonesty"
> - ⚠️ Similar meaning in [[wham]] (from [[Mushoku Tensei]])
