## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Very Frequent)
To tie or fasten something tightly with cord, rope, or other material; to restrain or hold in place.

> [!example] Examples
> - The warrior used energy beams to bind his opponent in place.
> - Bind the scroll with leather straps before traveling.
> - The chains bind him to the wall.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Frequent)
To hold together; to unite or cause to remain connected.

> [!example] Examples
> - Friendship binds the group of fighters together.
> - The contract binds both parties to their agreement.
> - A shared enemy binds the allies in common cause.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Frequent)
A constraint, obligation, or difficult situation that restricts freedom.

> [!example] Examples
> - The hero found himself in a bind when betrayed by his ally.
> - Breaking free from that bind required great effort.
> - They were in a bind and had no good options.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *bindan*, related to Old Norse *binda* and German *binden*. Originally meant "to tie" or "to fasten," evolved to include metaphorical uses of constraint and obligation.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 is physical and literal, about tying or fastening
> - Sense 2 is abstract, about unity or obligation
> - Sense 3 emphasizes predicament or constrained situation
> - "In a bind" is a common idiom meaning in a difficult situation
> - Binding agreements/contracts are legally enforceable
