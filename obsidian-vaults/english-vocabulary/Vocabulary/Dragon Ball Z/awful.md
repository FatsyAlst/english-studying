## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (Common)
Very bad or unpleasant; terrible; poor in quality; causing feelings of fear, shock, or disgust.

> [!example] Examples
> - The awful weather prevented the outdoor event.
> - That's an awful idea.
> - He felt awful after hearing the terrible news.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Archaic/Literary)
Inspiring awe or fear; worthy of reverence or dread.

> [!example] Examples
> - The awful majesty of the mountains (archaic usage)
> - An awful power (archaic literary usage)

## 📜 Etymology
From "awe" + "-ful" (suffix meaning full of). Originally meant "full of awe" or "awe-inspiring" (archaic). Over time, the meaning shifted to "inspiring negative feelings" like fear or horror, and eventually to the modern sense of "very bad" or "unpleasant."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Modern sense: "very bad" or "unpleasant" (most common)
> - Archaic sense: "inspiring awe" (rarely used in modern English)
> - "Awfully" is often used as an intensifier (awfully sorry, awfully kind)
> - Synonyms: terrible, dreadful, bad, poor, unpleasant, disgusting
> - Very common in everyday conversation
> - Slightly informal but widely accepted in all contexts
