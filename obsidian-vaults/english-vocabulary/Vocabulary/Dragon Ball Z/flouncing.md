## 📖 Definition
*Verb (gerund/present participle)* — Less Common

To move in a way that shows anger, frustration, or annoyance through exaggerated body movements, typically with jerky or abrupt motions; to move restlessly or impatiently.

> [!example] Examples
> - He's flouncing around the arena, angry about the decision
> - She flounced out of the room in frustration
> - The fighter was flouncing on the mat, upset with the referee

## 📜 Etymology
Origin uncertain, but possibly from Scandinavian sources. First recorded use in English around 1520s. May be related to "flounder" (to struggle). Some suggest influence from "flange" or similar motion-describing words.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Describes exaggerated, emotional movements
> - Often implies petulance or anger
> - Can describe physical fabric movement (less common modern usage)
> - Tends to have slightly negative or dismissive connotations
> - More common in British English
