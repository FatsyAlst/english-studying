## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Common)
A feeling of alarm, anxiety, or disappointment caused by something unexpected or unwelcome.

> [!example] Examples
> - To the fighters' dismay, their opponent was far stronger than expected.
> - She watched with dismay as her plan fell apart.
> - The news of his defeat filled them with dismay.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To cause someone to feel alarm, anxiety, or disappointment; to disconcert or disappoint.

> [!example] Examples
> - The warrior's immense power dismayed his challengers.
> - Nothing could dismay Goku, no matter how strong his opponent.
> - The unexpected transformation dismayed everyone watching.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French "desmayer" meaning "to lose one's courage." From "des-" (reversal) + "mayer" (to be able or have power), related to "may" meaning "might" or "power." Combined: "to deprive of power/courage."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - More formal and literary than "surprise" or "disappointment"
> - Suggests a stronger negative emotion
> - Often used in narrative contexts
> - "To one's dismay" is a common phrase structure
> - Past participle "dismayed" can function as adjective
> - Implies concern about consequences, not just disappointment
