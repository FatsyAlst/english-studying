## 📖 Definition
*adjective* (Common)

Easily deceived or tricked; prone to believing false information without questioning it; credulous.

> [!example] Examples
> - Don't be so gullible; that's obviously a scam.
> - Young children are often more gullible than adults.
> - He was too gullible to notice the deception.

## 📜 Etymology
From "gull" (to deceive or cheat, from the gull bird which was considered foolish) + "-ible" (suffix meaning capable of being). First recorded in the 17th century. The word evolved from the practice of calling foolish people "gulls" (like the bird).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Negative connotation; describes someone who is easily deceived.
> - Related to credibility and critical thinking.
> - Noun form: "gullibility" (the quality of being gullible).
> - Similar to "naive" or "credulous" but with stronger implication of being foolish.
> - Often used to warn against or criticize excessive trust or naivety.
