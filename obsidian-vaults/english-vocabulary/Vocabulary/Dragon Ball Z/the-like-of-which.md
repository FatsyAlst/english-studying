## 📖 Definition
*phrase* (Formal/Literary)

Used to indicate something of the same kind or nature; similar things or examples; often emphasizing the exceptional or unprecedented nature of what is being described.

> [!example] Examples
> - It was a disaster the like of which had never been seen before.
> - A power the like of which no one had ever encountered.
> - She had never experienced a love the like of which she felt now.

---
## 📜 Etymology
"Like" derives from Old English *gelic* (same, similar). The phrase "the like of which" is a formal construction combining "the" + "like" (noun, meaning a similar thing) + "of" + "which," creating an archaic or literary structure.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Formal and somewhat archaic construction.
> - Often used in epic or dramatic contexts to emphasize uniqueness.
> - Modern alternative: "the likes of" (more colloquial) or "such as had never been seen."
> - Emphasizes something extraordinary or unprecedented.
> - Common in classical literature, fantasy, and dramatic narratives.
> - Grammatically, it functions as a relative clause.
