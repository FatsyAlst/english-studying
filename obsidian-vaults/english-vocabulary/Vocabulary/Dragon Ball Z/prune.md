## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (common)
A dried plum, used as a food and natural laxative.

> [!example] Examples
> - The elderly fighter ate a prune for a healthy snack.
> - A bowl of prunes was offered at the feast.
> - Prunes are known for their health benefits.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (common)
To cut off or remove branches, stems, or other parts from a plant to improve growth or shape.

> [!example] Examples
> - The gardener will prune the trees in spring.
> - You should prune the dead branches to help the tree grow stronger.
> - He pruned away the damaged limbs after the storm.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (common)
To remove or eliminate unnecessary parts from something; to reduce or simplify.

> [!example] Examples
> - We need to prune unnecessary details from the plan.
> - She pruned the list down to the most important items.
> - Prune the excess from your training routine.

## 📜 Etymology
The noun "prune" comes from Old French "prune" and Latin "pruna," originally from Greek "prounon." The verb "prune" is derived from the noun and Old French "pruner," originally meaning to trim fruit trees.

## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Noun and verb share etymology but are distinct meanings
> - Pruning is common in gardening and plant care
> - Figuratively used to mean simplification or removal of excess
