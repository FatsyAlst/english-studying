## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A word, formula, or incantation believed to have magical power; a magical charm or curse.

> [!example] Examples
> - The witch cast a spell on the castle.
> - Under her spell, he couldn't refuse anything.
> - Breaking the spell required true love's kiss.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To write or name the letters of a word in order.

> [!example] Examples
> - How do you spell "bureaucracy"?
> - Can you spell your name for me?

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common, Figurative)
A state of enchantment or fascination; a period of influence.

> [!example] Examples
> - She fell under his spell.
> - The spell of the music held the audience captive.
> - A cold spell lasted all winter.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Common)
A period of time, usually short; a turn or shift.

> [!example] Examples
> - He worked a long spell at the factory.
> - After a brief spell in London, she returned home.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *spel* (story, speech), from Proto-Germanic *spellaz*. The magical sense developed through association with mysterious utterances and incantations. The verb sense (to spell out letters) came from the noun, meaning to explain letter by letter.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Multiple distinct meanings: magic formula, writing letters, fascination, period of time.
> - "Cast a spell" = to bewitch or enchant (magical or figurative).
> - "Under someone's spell" = fascinated or controlled by them.
> - "Spell out" = explain something clearly and explicitly.
> - British English: "spelt" (past tense); American English: "spelled" (more common).
