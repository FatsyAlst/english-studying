## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (common)
To heat a liquid until it reaches 100°C (212°F) at sea level, causing it to turn to steam with large bubbles rapidly forming and bursting.

> [!example] Examples
> - The water began to boil in the kettle.
> - She boiled the rice in salted water.
> - Boil the vegetables for exactly ten minutes.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (common)
To be very hot and agitated, with rapid movement of bubbles; to seethe.

> [!example] Examples
> - The lava was boiling inside the volcano.
> - The cauldron boiled with mysterious liquid.
> - The water boiled furiously as it heated.

### Sense 3 — *verb (figurative)* (common)
To be extremely angry or upset; to feel intense emotion about to explode outward.

> [!example] Examples
> - His anger was boiling just beneath the surface.
> - She boiled with rage at the injustice.
> - His blood boiled when he learned the truth.

### Sense 4 — *verb (figurative)* (common)
To reduce or condense a complex idea to its essential parts or core meaning.

> [!example] Examples
> - The matter boils down to a single question.
> - It boils down to whether you trust him or not.
> - Everything boils down to power in this world.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French "boillir," from Latin "bullire" (to bubble, seethe). Originally referred to the bubbling action of heated liquid. The metaphorical sense (to be angry) emerged in the 16th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 is literal, referring to heating liquid to its boiling point
> - Sense 3 (emotional) is very common in action and dramatic contexts
> - "Boil over" means to overflow physically or emotionally explode
> - "Boil down to" is an idiom meaning to reduce to the essentials
> - "Make someone's blood boil" = make someone extremely angry
