## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb* (informal)
To go away; to leave; to stop bothering someone (often used as a rude command).

> [!example] Examples
> - Bug off! I don't want to hear your excuses!
> - He told the annoying fighter to bug off.
> - She was bothering everyone, so they told her to bug off.

### Sense 2 — *phrasal verb* (informal)
To stop bothering or annoying someone; to cease pestering.

> [!example] Examples
> - Bug off with your constant questions!
> - If you don't bug off, we'll fight right now.
> - He kept asking for money until I told him to bug off.

## 📜 Etymology
Phrasal verb combining "bug" (to annoy or pester, possibly from 16th century usage meaning to infest like insects, or from "bugaboo") and "off" (from Old English "of"). The phrase emerged in informal English in the 20th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Informal and somewhat rude
> - Usually used as an imperative (command): "bug off!"
> - Similar to "get lost" or "leave me alone"
> - Non-separable phrasal verb
> - Common in casual or confrontational dialogue
