## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective/phrasal verb* (very frequent)
Exhausted; tired from physical exertion or effort

> [!example] Examples
> - After the intense battle, the warrior was worn out
> - He was completely worn out from fighting
> - They looked worn out and defeated
> - The worn out fighters could barely stand

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (frequent)
Damaged or deteriorated from heavy use or age; shabby

> [!example] Examples
> - His worn out armor showed signs of many battles
> - The worn out equipment needed replacement
> - The worn out clothes were tattered

## 📜 Etymology
Combination of "worn" (past participle of wear) and "out." "Wear" comes from Old English "werian" meaning to carry or have on one's body. The phrasal combination "wear out" developed to mean both physical deterioration and mental/physical exhaustion.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can describe both exhaustion from effort and physical deterioration from use
> - Common in descriptions of battles and their aftermath
> - As an adjective, functions as a past participle phrase but is treated as single adjective
> - Both literal and figurative uses are common
> - ⚠️ Similar meaning in [[whacked]] (from [[Mushoku Tensei]])
