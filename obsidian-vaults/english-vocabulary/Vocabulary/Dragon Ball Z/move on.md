## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Phrasal Verb* (Most Common)
To continue moving forward or to another place; to stop staying in one location and go elsewhere.

> [!example] Examples
> - We should move on to the next village
> - After resting, they moved on down the road
> - Let's move on from here
> - The army moved on to the next objective

---
### Sense 2 — *Phrasal Verb* (Common)
To progress or advance in life or a project; to stop focusing on something past and continue with something new.

> [!example] Examples
> - After losing, he had to move on
> - Let's move on from that topic
> - You need to move on with your life
> - It's time to move on to the next challenge

---
### Sense 3 — *Phrasal Verb* (Less Common)
To end a romantic relationship; to stop being interested in someone.

> [!example] Examples
> - I'm going to move on from this relationship
> - He needed to move on from his past
> - She decided to move on

## 📜 Etymology
"Move" comes from Old French "mover" from Latin "movere". "On" is a preposition indicating continuation or direction. "Move on" developed as a phrasal verb with multiple related meanings based on physical and metaphorical movement.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Literal sense: physically relocating to another location
> - Figurative sense: progressing past an obstacle or issue
> - Can be positive (moving forward) or resigned (accepting and continuing)
> - Very common in casual speech and writing
> - Used in many contexts: travel, emotions, projects, relationships
