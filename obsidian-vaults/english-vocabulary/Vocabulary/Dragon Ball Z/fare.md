## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (common)
The price of a journey on public transportation such as a bus, train, or plane.

> [!example] Examples
> - The bus fare has increased this month.
> - He paid the train fare to travel to the tournament.
> - Airfare to distant lands is expensive.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (common)
Food or a meal, especially as served in a restaurant or at an event.

> [!example] Examples
> - The feast offered a variety of delicious fare.
> - Simple fare was served at the training hall.
> - Exotic fare from different regions was available.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (common)
To get along; to manage or perform (used with adverbs like "well" or "poorly").

> [!example] Examples
> - How did you fare in the tournament?
> - She fared well despite the difficult odds.
> - They fared poorly without proper training.

## 📜 Etymology
The noun "fare" comes from Old English "faru" (a going, a journey) and also from Old English "fer" (food). The verb evolved from the noun sense. All relate to travel and journey concepts.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (price) is common for transportation costs
> - Sense 2 (food) is more literary or formal
> - Sense 3 (to manage) is often used in questions: "How did you fare?"
> - Present tense "fares" sounds like "fairs" but means something entirely different
