## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (common)
Disgusting or repulsive; very offensive to the senses or to good taste.

> [!example] Examples
> - That's gross! I don't want to see it.
> - Gross behavior is not acceptable in class.
> - He made a gross joke that offended everyone.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (formal)
Glaring or obvious, especially in a negative way; very clear and unacceptable.

> [!example] Examples
> - A gross violation of the rules
> - Gross negligence in the investigation
> - That was a gross error in judgment.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (accounting/business)
The total amount of money before deductions, taxes, or expenses.

> [!example] Examples
> - The gross for the quarter was $2 million.
> - Her gross salary is $50,000.
> - The store's gross revenue increased this year.

### Sense 4 — *verb* (business)
To earn as a total before deductions; to make (a specified amount) in total profit.

> [!example] Examples
> - The film grossed $100 million at the box office.
> - Our company grossed $5 million last year.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French gros (large, thick), from Latin grossus. The sense of "disgusting" developed in English. The business sense comes from "total" meaning "the whole thing."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - The adjective sense (disgusting) is very common in casual speech
> - Business sense is common in financial contexts
> - "Gross out" is a phrasal verb meaning to disgust someone
> - "Gross" as a noun can mean 144 items (12 dozen)
