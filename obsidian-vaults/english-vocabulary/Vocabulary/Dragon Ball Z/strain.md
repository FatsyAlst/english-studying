## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A force or pressure that stretches, pulls, or pushes something to its limit, often causing damage or deformation.

> [!example] Examples
> - The bridge couldn't withstand the strain of the heavy load.
> - The rope snapped under the strain.
> - Emotional strain can lead to burnout.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
A state of worry, tension, or fatigue caused by too much pressure or effort.

> [!example] Examples
> - The long hours put a strain on their marriage.
> - He's under a lot of strain at work.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common)
A type or variety of something, such as a breed of animal, plant, or virus.

> [!example] Examples
> - This is a high-yield strain of wheat.
> - The flu strain is highly contagious this year.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Less Common) — _literary_
A melody or tune, especially in poetry or music.

> [!example] Examples
> - The strains of music filled the air.

### Sense 5 — *verb* (Common)
To injure or damage by stretching or overworking (muscle, body part).

> [!example] Examples
> - She strained her back lifting the box.
> - Don't strain yourself carrying that heavy bag.

### Sense 6 — *verb* (Common)
To make a great effort, using all one's strength or senses.

> [!example] Examples
> - He strained to hear the distant voice.
> - Strain every nerve to finish on time.

### Sense 7 — *verb* (Common)
To separate solid material from a liquid by passing through a sieve or filter.

> [!example] Examples
> - Strain the pasta before adding the sauce.
> - Strain the soup to remove lumps.

---
Verb from Old French *estreindre* 'bind tightly,' from Latin *stringere* 'draw tight' (Proto-Indo-European *streig-* 'stroke, press'). Noun senses from mid-16th c.; 'lineage/breed' from Old English *strion* 'gain, procreation.'

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - Highly versatile; context determines literal vs. figurative.
> - Physical sense: injury to muscles/body; emotional sense: stress; biological sense: variety/type.
> - Verb 'strain' for cooking (filtering) is very common in recipes.
