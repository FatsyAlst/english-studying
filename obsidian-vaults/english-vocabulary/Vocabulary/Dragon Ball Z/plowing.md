## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
Turning over soil in a field with a plow to prepare it for planting.

> [!example] Examples
> - The farmer was plowing his field early in the morning.
> - After plowing, they planted the seeds in neat rows.
> - Tractors are used for plowing large areas quickly.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
Moving forcefully through something in a way that pushes it aside, often with difficulty.

> [!example] Examples
> - The ship was plowing through the heavy waves.
> - He plowed through the snow to reach the house.
> - The car plowed into the fence after losing control.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
Working steadily through a large amount of work, information, or tasks.

> [!example] Examples
> - She's been plowing through the reports all day.
> - I have a lot of emails to plow through before lunch.

### Sense 4 — *verb* (Less Common)
Clearing snow from roads or areas with a snowplow.

> [!example] Examples
> - The city trucks were plowing the streets after the blizzard.
> - They started plowing the driveway at dawn.

---
**Pronunciation:** `[plaʊ]`

### *noun* (Common)
A tool for turning over soil; a device for removing snow from roads.

> [!example] Examples
> - The farmer used a wooden plow drawn by oxen.

### *verb* (Most Common)
To turn over soil with a plow; to move forcefully through something; to work through steadily.

> [!example] Examples
> - We plow the field in spring.
> - The ship plowed through the waves.

From Late Old English 'plōg/plōh' ('plow, plowland'), borrowed from a Scandinavian source (Proto-Germanic *plōgō-); replaced earlier 'sulh' (furrow). Ultimate origin unknown, but related to words for plowing in other languages.

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - British spelling is 'ploughing' (same pronunciation); both are correct everywhere now.
> - Figurative uses like 'plowing through work' are very common in informal English.
