## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Verb* (Common)
To prevent someone from succeeding in a plan or intention; to thwart or frustrate someone's efforts; to defeat or block.

> [!example] Examples
> - His strategy managed to foil the opponent's devastating attack.
> - The hero foiled the villain's final plan.
> - No power could foil his determination.

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Less Common)
A thin sheet of metal (especially aluminum); a contrast that emphasizes distinctive characteristics; a light sword used in fencing.

> [!example] Examples
> - He wrapped the supplies in protective foil.
> - The gentle character served as a foil to the fierce warrior.

## 📜 Etymology
The verb comes from Old French *fouler* (to trample, press), from Latin *fullo* (fuller, one who fulls cloth). The noun (metal sheet) has a different origin from Old French *foille*, from Latin *folium* (leaf). The "dramatic foil" sense developed later in literary contexts.

## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Verb: to prevent success; to thwart
> - Noun: thin metal sheet; character that contrasts with another
> - "Foil someone's plans" is a common idiomatic expression
> - Literary use: a character who contrasts with the protagonist
> - Different pronunciations for different senses
> - Related: foiled (adjective), foiler (one who foils)
