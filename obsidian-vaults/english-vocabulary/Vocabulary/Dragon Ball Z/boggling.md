## 📖 Definition
Causing great surprise, astonishment, or bewilderment; overwhelming or astounding (often in 'mind-boggling').

---
**Pronunciation:** `/ˈbɒɡ.əl/`

### *verb* (archaic)
To hesitate or be astonished; to shy away in fear.

> [!example] Examples
> - The old verb boggle meant to hesitate in astonishment.

---
| Part of Speech | Forms |
|----------------|-------|
| Noun | boggle, boggler |
| Verb | boggle, boggles, boggled, boggling |
| Adjective | boggling |

---
- Mind-boggling (complexity/speed/cost/sums)
- Absolutely/completely boggling
- Boggling the mind/imagination

---
Verb 'boggle' from late 16th century Scots, probably from *bogle* 'hobgoblin, spectre' (Middle English *bugge* 'bugbear,' possibly Scandinavian). 'Mind-boggling' coined mid-20th century for 'astounding'.

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - Mostly in 'mind-boggling' (informal, emphatic for incredible).
> - Implies overwhelming scale/effect.
> - Positive/neutral for amazement.
> - Synonyms: astounding, staggering.
