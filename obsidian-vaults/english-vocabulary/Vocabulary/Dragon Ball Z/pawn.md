## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A chess piece of the lowest rank, each player starting with eight pawns.

> [!example] Examples
> - In chess, a pawn can be promoted to a queen if it reaches the opposite end.
> - She sacrificed a pawn to gain a strategic advantage.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common, Figurative)
A person who is used or manipulated by someone more powerful; someone of little importance in a larger scheme.

> [!example] Examples
> - He realized he was just a pawn in their game.
> - They treated him as a mere pawn, using him for their own purposes.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To pledge or give something as security for a loan; to deposit something at a pawnshop.

> [!example] Examples
> - He pawned his watch to get money for food.
> - She pawned her grandmother's ring reluctantly.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *paon* (foot soldier), from Latin *pedō* (foot). The term referred to common soldiers in medieval warfare, who were the lowest rank. Later applied to the chess piece (introduced to Europe in the 6th century), and eventually to the verb meaning "to pledge as security."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Figuratively, "a pawn" = someone manipulated or insignificant in larger events.
> - "Pawn shop" = a store where people pledge items for loans.
> - In chess, a pawn can be "promoted" to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight if it reaches the opposite end.
> - The verb form is common in historical/modern contexts of financial desperation.
