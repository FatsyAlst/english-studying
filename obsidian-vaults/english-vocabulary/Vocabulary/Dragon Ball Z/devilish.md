## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Mischievous, playful, or roguish in a charming or naughty way; impishly amusing or clever.

> [!example] Examples
> - He had a devilish grin that hinted at his prank.
> - Her devilish sense of humor always made everyone laugh.
> - The children had a devilish plan for the party.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
Evil, cruel, sinister, or wicked, like the Devil; diabolical or malicious.

> [!example] Examples
> - The villain had a devilish plot to take over the kingdom.
> - His devilish laughter echoed through the dark castle.

### Sense 3 — *adverb* (Common) — _informal_
Extremely or very; used as an intensifier (often archaic or literary).

> [!example] Examples
> - It's devilish hard to solve this puzzle.
> - The test was devilish difficult.

Late 15th century from 'devil' + '-ish'; from Old English *dēofol* (Latin *diabolus*, Greek *diabolos* 'slanderer'). Originally 'diabolical'; mischievous sense from circa 1600.

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - Often positive/amusing (mischievous) in modern use; can be negative for evil/sinister meanings.
> - The adjective 'devilish' is much more common than the adverb form in contemporary English.
> - Related word 'devilry' means playful mischief or devious behavior.
> - 'Devil-may-care' describes a reckless, carefree attitude.
