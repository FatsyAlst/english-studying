## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common) — _Scottish, Northern English_
Very small in size or amount; tiny or diminutive.

> [!example] Examples
> - Just a wee drop of milk in my tea, please.
> - She's just a wee lass with a big smile.
> - I'll be a wee bit late tonight.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
Very early, especially referring to hours of the morning (the wee hours).

> [!example] Examples
> - We danced into the wee hours of the morning.
> - I woke up in the wee hours with a start.
> - They worked through the wee small hours to finish the project.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common) — _colloquial, UK, child-friendly_
Urine or an act of urinating; a euphemistic term used especially with children.

> [!example] Examples
> - I need a wee before we go.
> - The toddler had a wee on the floor.

### Sense 4 — *verb* (Common) — _colloquial, UK_
To urinate; to pass urine.

> [!example] Examples
> - Do you need to wee, little one?
> - The baby weed all over my clothes.

Adjective from Middle English *wey/weȳe* 'small amount/quantity,' from Old English *wǣġ* 'weight,' Proto-Germanic *wēgo* 'scales, weight.' Originally Scots/Scottish English, now widespread in British Isles. Noun/verb (urine) from 19th century childish euphemism.

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - Charming/informal, especially with Scottish flavor; affectionate for small or cute things.
> - Urine senses are child-friendly/euphemistic, commonly used when talking to young children.
> - 'Wee hours' is a common expression in all varieties of English for very early morning (e.g., 2-5 AM).
> - The adjective sense is widespread in Scottish, Northern Irish, and Northern English dialects.
