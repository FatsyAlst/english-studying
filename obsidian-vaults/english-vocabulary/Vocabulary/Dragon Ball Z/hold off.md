## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb* (common)
To resist or restrain someone or something; to prevent from advancing.

> [!example] Examples
> - The weakest fighter tried to hold off the powerful enemy.
> - Can you hold off the attack while I gather my strength?
> - We need to hold off the invasion until reinforcements arrive.

### Sense 2 — *phrasal verb* (common)
To delay or refrain from doing something; to postpone.

> [!example] Examples
> - Hold off on making a decision until you have more information.
> - I'm holding off on my final attack to see his true power.
> - We held off announcing the news until everyone was present.

## 📜 Etymology
Phrasal verb combining "hold" (from Old English "haldan") and "off" (from Old English "of"). The combination emerged in Middle English to express both physical resistance and temporal delay.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Separable phrasal verb: "hold off the enemy" or "hold the enemy off"
> - Sense 2 is often followed by "on": "hold off on something"
> - Common in combat and action contexts
