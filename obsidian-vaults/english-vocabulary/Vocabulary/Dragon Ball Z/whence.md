## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adverb* (archaic/formal)
From what place or source; from which; from where (archaic or poetic form of "where from").

> [!example] Examples
> - "Whence did you learn such extraordinary power?" asked King Kai.
> - He wondered whence the mysterious warrior had arrived.
> - The master questioned whence his students derived their newfound strength.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "hwanon," derived from the interrogative base "hwa-" (who, what). The "-nce" suffix indicates origin or source, similar to "thence" (from there) and "hence" (from here). It is archaic and rarely used in modern English.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Archaic and formal/poetic tone; rarely used in modern conversational English
> - Primarily found in literature, formal writing, or dialogue meant to evoke older language
> - Modern equivalents: "where from," "from where," or "from which"
> - Often used in questions to inquire about origins or sources
