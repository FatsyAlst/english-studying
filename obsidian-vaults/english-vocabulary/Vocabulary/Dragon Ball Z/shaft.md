## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Very Frequent)
A long, narrow rod or pole; typically the handle or main body of a tool or weapon.

> [!example] Examples
> - The spear's shaft was made of hardened steel.
> - The arrow's shaft was perfectly straight.
> - He gripped the shaft of his weapon firmly.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Frequent)
A vertical passage or opening in a building, mine, or other structure.

> [!example] Examples
> - The elevator shaft extended from the basement to the roof.
> - Coal miners descended into the mine shaft daily.
> - Light poured down through the ventilation shaft.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Less Frequent)
A beam or ray of light.

> [!example] Examples
> - A shaft of sunlight streamed through the window.
> - Golden shafts of light illuminated the chamber.
> - The shaft of moonlight revealed the hidden path.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *sceaft*, related to Old Norse *skapt* and German *Schaft*. Originally meant "spear" or "pole," evolving to describe any long rod or cylindrical passage.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 is common in tools and weapons contexts
> - Sense 2 is technical, used in architecture and mining
> - Sense 3 is poetic or literary, often used metaphorically
> - Each sense is distinct but related conceptually (long, narrow structures)
