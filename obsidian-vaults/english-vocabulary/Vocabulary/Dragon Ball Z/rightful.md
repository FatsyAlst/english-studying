## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Having a just or legally established claim; legitimate. Used to describe a position, claim, or ownership that is morally or legally correct.

> [!example] Examples
> - The property should be returned to its rightful owner.
> - He will be given his rightful place in the history books.
> - She claimed her rightful inheritance.

## 📜 Etymology
Old English period (pre-1150), formed from *right* (noun) + *-ful* (suffix meaning "full of" or "having the qualities of"). Originally emphasized moral correctness and justice, maintaining this core meaning through centuries while expanding to include legal legitimacy.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Most frequently modifies "owner" — *rightful owner* is by far the most common collocation.
> - Carries moral weight beyond mere legality — implies that something *properly* or *deservedly* belongs to someone.
> - Often used when something has been wrongly taken or displaced, suggesting restoration of proper order.
> - Register is neutral to formal; common in legal contexts, property discussions, and historical/political discourse.
