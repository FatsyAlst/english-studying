## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (common)
To make someone feel uninterested or tired by being dull or unengaging.

> [!example] Examples
> - The weak fighter's style bored the spectators.
> - Long speeches bore most of the audience.
> - Don't bore me with details; just fight!

### Sense 2 — *verb* (common)
To drill, pierce, or make a hole through something.

> [!example] Examples
> - The energy beam bored through the mountain.
> - He bored a hole in the wall with his power.
> - The blast bored straight through the armor.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (common)
A person or thing that is boring, dull, or uninteresting.

> [!example] Examples
> - That fighter is such a bore—no one wants to watch him.
> - The training routine became a bore after years of repetition.
> - He's no bore in a real fight; he's extremely skilled.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (common)
The diameter of the barrel of a gun, or the inner diameter of a cylindrical object.

> [!example] Examples
> - The cannon's bore was quite large.
> - The rifle's bore was perfectly aligned.
> - A larger bore creates a more powerful blast.

## 📜 Etymology
Sense 1 (to make weary) comes from Old French "borer" and possibly from Celtic sources. Sense 2 (to drill/pierce) comes from Old English "borian," related to "bur" (to burrow). Sense 3 and 4 (noun forms) derive from the verb forms.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 is about emotional/mental tiredness
> - Sense 2 is literal, physical piercing or drilling
> - The noun "bore" (sense 3) refers to a tedious person/thing
> - Past tense "bored" is different from present "boring"
> - Can also mean "to endure patiently" in archaic usage: "bore witness"
