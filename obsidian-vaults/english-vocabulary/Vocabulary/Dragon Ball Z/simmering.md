## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb (present participle)* (common)
Cooking or heating at a temperature just below boiling, with small bubbles forming gently on the surface.

> [!example] Examples
> - The soup has been simmering on the stove for hours.
> - Keep the water simmering while you add the ingredients.
> - The broth was simmering over the fire.

### Sense 2 — *verb (present participle)* (figurative)
Experiencing a strong emotion that is barely being kept under control; to be on the verge of emotional explosion.

> [!example] Examples
> - His anger was simmering beneath the surface.
> - The tension between them had been simmering for weeks.
> - She was simmering with rage, ready to strike at any moment.

### Sense 3 — *adjective* (figurative)
Characterized by suppressed or barely contained emotion; brewing or developing.

> [!example] Examples
> - A simmering conflict threatened to boil over.
> - The simmering resentment finally erupted into battle.
> - There was a simmering anticipation in the crowd.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle English "simeren," possibly from Old French "soumire" or related to the sound of gentle boiling. May also be related to "summer" (to swim) or imitative of the sound of simmering liquid.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 is literal and cooking-related
> - Sense 2 and 3 are figurative, often describing emotions or conflicts
> - "Simmer down" is an idiom meaning to calm down or become less angry
> - Common in both literal and metaphorical contexts
