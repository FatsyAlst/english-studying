## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (common)
To bend, twist, or distort something out of its original shape; to become warped.

> [!example] Examples
> - The intense heat warped the metal armor.
> - The blast warped the landscape beyond recognition.
> - His perception of reality became warped after the trauma.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (common)
To transport or move something instantly from one place to another (in science fiction/fantasy contexts).

> [!example] Examples
> - The teleportation ability allowed him to warp to any location.
> - She warped across the battlefield in an instant.
> - The portal warped them to a different dimension.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (common)
A distortion or bend in something; the state of being twisted or deformed.

> [!example] Examples
> - The warp in the wood made the door difficult to close.
> - A warp in the fabric of space itself occurred.
> - The warp and weave of the cosmic energy was visible.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (common)
In weaving, the lengthwise threads on a loom.

> [!example] Examples
> - The weaver carefully arranged the warp threads.
> - The warp and weft create the fabric's structure.
> - Each warp thread must be perfectly taut.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English "wearp" (to throw) and Old Norse "varp" (casting). Originally referred to the lengthwise threads on a loom (from the act of warping/setting up the loom), later extended to mean distortion or bending.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (bend/distort) is common in physical and metaphorical uses
> - Sense 2 (teleport) is primarily used in science fiction and fantasy
> - "Warped" can describe both physical distortion and moral/mental corruption
> - Weaving sense is technical and specialized
