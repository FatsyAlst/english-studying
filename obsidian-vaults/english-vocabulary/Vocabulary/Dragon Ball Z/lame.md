## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (High Frequency)
Lacking power or effectiveness; weak or feeble; not impressive or convincing.

> [!example] Examples
> - That attack was lame compared to his usual power level.
> - His excuse for losing was pretty lame.
> - The strategy seemed lame against such a strong opponent.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (Medium Frequency)
(Of a person or animal) unable to walk normally; crippled or limping; physically disabled.

> [!example] Examples
> - The injured warrior walked with a lame gait.
> - A lame horse cannot race effectively.
> - He limped away with a lame leg from the battle.

### Sense 3 — *Adjective* (Medium Frequency)
Dull, boring, or not fashionable; uncool or unfashionable.

> [!example] Examples
> - The party was totally lame without any good music.
> - His outfit looked kind of lame compared to everyone else's.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *lama*, from Proto-Germanic *lamaz*. Related to Old Saxon *lam* and Old Norse *lamr*. The original meaning referred to physical disability.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Multiple distinct meanings: physically disabled, weak/ineffective, and uncool
> - "Lame duck" is an idiom for a person who is ineffectual
> - Informal usage to describe something boring or uncool is common in contemporary English
> - The physical disability meaning is less common in modern casual speech
> - Context is important to understand which sense is being used
