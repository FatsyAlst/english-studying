## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To produce or gather something, especially feelings, strength, or courage, with effort or determination.

> [!example] Examples
> - She managed to muster the courage to ask him to the movies.
> - They pushed the car with all the strength they could muster.
> - I shouted with all the lung power I could muster.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To gather together as a group or force, especially in preparation for action; to assemble troops for duty or inspection.

> [!example] Examples
> - The troops mustered on the hill.
> - The general mustered his troops.
> - The navy could muster 44 warships.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To gather together enough of something, such as support or votes.

> [!example] Examples
> - Opponents are unlikely to muster enough votes to override the veto.
> - They mustered at least 23 votes to defeat the bill.
> - The campaign mustered widespread public support.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Less Common)
A formal military inspection or assembly of troops.

> [!example] Examples
> - Call out the troops to stand muster.
> - If anyone failed to attend the muster, heavy fines could be imposed.
> - The annual muster of the local militia drew crowds of onlookers.

## 📜 Etymology
Late Middle English from Old French *moustrer* (verb), *moustre* (noun), from Latin *monstrare* ("to show"). Originally meant "to display" or "to show," particularly in a military context where troops were assembled for inspection.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - **"Pass muster"** — idiom meaning to be accepted as adequate or satisfactory. From military inspections where troops had to pass review.
> - **"Muster" vs. "muster up"** — both correct and interchangeable; "muster up" is slightly more emphatic and suggests greater effort.
> - More commonly used figuratively (gathering courage, strength) than literally (assembling troops).
> - Often pairs with modal verbs: *can muster*, *could muster*, *able to muster*.
> - Compare with [[strive]] — both involve summoning effort, but *muster* focuses on the gathering of resources while *strive* focuses on the sustained effort.
