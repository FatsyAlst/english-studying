## 📖 Definition
*conjunction/phrase* (Common)

Used to introduce a comparison or to suggest something that is not actually true; as if; in the manner of something hypothetical or unreal.

> [!example] Examples
> - He ran as though his life depended on it.
> - She looked at him as though he were a stranger.
> - It feels as though summer will never come.

---
## 📜 Etymology
"As" comes from Old English *als* (also, similarly), and "though" from Old English *þēah* (nevertheless, however). Together, "as though" literally means "in a manner similar to the case that" or "as if."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Introduces hypothetical or comparative clauses.
> - Often used to describe behavior, appearance, or feelings that are unreal or exaggerated.
> - "As if" and "as though" are essentially interchangeable; "as if" is slightly more common in American English.
> - Can express counterfactual scenarios (things not actually true).
> - Common in descriptive and narrative writing.
