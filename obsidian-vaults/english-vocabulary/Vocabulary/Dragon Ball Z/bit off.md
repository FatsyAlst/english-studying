## 📖 Definition
*Phrasal Verb* — (Common)
To undertake or attempt something that is too difficult or more than one can handle; to take on more responsibility than one can manage.

> [!example] Examples
> - Vegeta bit off more than he could chew when he challenged Goku.
> - The young fighters bit off more than they could handle in the tournament.
> - He bit off more than he could swallow by fighting four opponents at once.

---
## 📜 Etymology
Idiom combining literal "bite off" (to remove by biting) with "chew" to create the metaphor of taking in too much to process. The phrase dates to at least the 1600s in English.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Common idiom with physical imagery that translates well to action contexts
> - Always used with "more than one can chew/handle/swallow"
> - Implies overconfidence or miscalculation
> - Often used as a warning or observation about someone's actions
> - Very fitting for anime battle scenes and challenges
> - Can be used in past tense "bit off"
