## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *phrasal verb/adjective* (very frequent)
Bound or restrained with rope, chains, or other materials; physically constrained

> [!example] Examples
> - The prisoner was tied up with heavy chains
> - His hands were tied up behind his back
> - They tied him up to prevent escape
> - He was left tied up on the battlefield

### Sense 2 — *phrasal verb/adjective* (frequent)
Occupied or unable to leave; busy or committed to something

> [!example] Examples
> - I'm tied up in meetings all day
> - He's tied up with family matters
> - She's tied up with important business
> - I'm tied up at the moment, can I call you back?

### Sense 3 — *phrasal verb* (less frequent)
To complete or conclude something; to finish up

> [!example] Examples
> - Tie up the loose ends of the plan
> - They tied up the details of the agreement
> - Let's tie up the remaining issues

## 📜 Etymology
"Tie" comes from Old English "tegan" and Proto-Germanic, meaning to bind or fasten. The phrasal "tie up" originally meant literal binding, later extended to mean being occupied or bound by commitment.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can describe both physical restraint and being busy/occupied
> - In action contexts, usually refers to physical captivity or restraint
> - "Be tied up" is common to indicate being busy
> - Often appears in descriptions of prisoners or captured characters
