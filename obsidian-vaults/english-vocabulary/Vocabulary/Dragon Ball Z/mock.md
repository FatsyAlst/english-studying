## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (frequent)
To ridicule, tease, or scoff at someone; to laugh at someone in a cruel or contemptuous manner.

> [!example] Examples
> - Don't mock his efforts; he's trying his best.
> - They mocked his power level before the battle.
> - He mocked her weakness with cruel laughter.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (frequent)
Not genuine or real; imitation or simulated; pretend.

> [!example] Examples
> - This is a mock battle, not a real fight.
> - The mock exam helped them prepare for the real test.
> - A mock sword made of wood.

### Sense 3 — *noun, "the mocks"* (Common — Informal, BrE)
Practice exams taken before the real ones (mock exams).

> [!example] Examples
> - I did badly in my mocks, but I passed the real exams.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French "moquer" (to mock, deceive), possibly from Germanic origin. The adjective sense (not genuine) emerged later, developed from the concept of mocking or counterfeiting something.

## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes

> - Can convey both amusement and cruelty depending on context
