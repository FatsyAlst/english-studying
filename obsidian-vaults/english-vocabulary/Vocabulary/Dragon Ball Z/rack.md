## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (common)
A framework, shelf, or stand for holding, storing, or displaying items; a structure with bars, rods, or shelves for organizing objects.

> [!example] Examples
> - The weapons were displayed on a rack in the martial arts training room.
> - The tournament featured a prize rack filled with valuable items.
> - Equipment racks lined the walls of the training facility.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (common)
To cause great pain, suffering, or distress to someone; to torment or torture (historical torture device).

> [!example] Examples
> - The intense battle racked his body with pain.
> - Guilt racked his mind as he contemplated his past decisions.
> - The warrior's muscles were racked with exhaustion after the training.

### Sense 3 — *phrasal verb: "rack one's brain"* (informal)
To think hard or strain one's memory; to search one's mind intensely for a solution or memory.

> [!example] Examples
> - He racked his brain trying to remember the ancient technique.
> - I racked my brain for the answer to the puzzle.
> - The fighters racked their brains for a winning strategy.

## 📜 Etymology
From Middle Dutch "rec" or "rek," meaning "a frame or stand." The word has roots in Germanic languages. The verb form (to torture) derives from a historical torture device called the "rack" that stretched the body. "Rack and ruin" (destruction) combines both the torture and decay meanings.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Sense 1 (noun) is most common in everyday usage
> - Sense 2 (verb meaning to torture) is more literary or formal
> - The phrasal verb "rack one's brain" is very common and idiomatic
> - "Racked with pain/guilt/exhaustion" is a common passive construction
> - Can be confused with "wrack" (destruction), though meanings overlap
