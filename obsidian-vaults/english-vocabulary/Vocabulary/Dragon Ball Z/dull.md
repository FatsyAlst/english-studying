## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Adjective* (High Frequency)
Not bright or shiny; lacking in luster or color; appearing flat or matte.

> [!example] Examples
> - The dull blade was ineffective in combat.
> - His eyes had a dull, lifeless appearance.
> - The dull sky suggested rain was coming.

### Sense 2 — *Adjective* (High Frequency)
Boring or uninteresting; lacking excitement or liveliness.

> [!example] Examples
> - The tournament was dull compared to last year's battles.
> - He found the long speech dull and repetitive.
> - Without Goku, the competition seemed dull.

### Sense 3 — *Adjective* (Medium Frequency)
Not sharp; blunt; not keenly felt or perceived.

> [!example] Examples
> - A dull blade can't cut properly.
> - He felt a dull pain in his shoulder after the match.
> - His dull hearing made him miss important announcements.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *dol* (stupid), related to Old Saxon *dol* and Old Norse *dullr*. The meaning evolved to include the sense of lacking brightness or sharpness.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Can refer to appearance, sensation, emotion, or intellectual state
> - "Never a dull moment" is a common idiom
> - Often used to describe both objects (blunt weapons) and experiences (boring events)
> - Can be used as a verb meaning to make something dull or less sharp
