## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
(+ adv./prep.) To appear as a large, vague shape, especially one that is frightening or threatening.

> [!example] Examples
> - A dark shape loomed up ahead of us.
> - Ghostly shapes loomed out of the fog.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To appear likely to happen soon, especially something unpleasant.

> [!example] Examples
> - There was a crisis looming.
> - The prospect of war loomed large.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Less Common)
A machine for weaving cloth.

> [!example] Examples
> - She works at a loom.

---
Verb: mid-16th c., possibly from Low German/Dutch (lōmen 'move slowly'; compare Frisian lūmen 'be weary'). Noun: Old English gelōma 'tool, implement' → Middle English lome.

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - Verb: negative/threatening connotation (danger, crisis looming).
> - Noun: technical (weaving); less frequent.
> - Idiom: 'loom large' = seem very important or threatening.
