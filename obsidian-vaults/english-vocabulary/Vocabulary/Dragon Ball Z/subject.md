## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Very Common)
A topic, theme, or matter under discussion or consideration.

> [!example] Examples
> - The subject of the tournament was martial arts excellence.
> - They discussed the subject of power levels.
> - What subject should we talk about next?

### Sense 2 — *Noun* (Common)
A field of study or a course taught in school or university.

> [!example] Examples
> - Math is my favorite subject in school.
> - He excels in the subject of physics.
> - The subjects offered include science, history, and language.

### Sense 3 — *Noun* (Common)
In grammar, the word or phrase that performs the action in a sentence.

> [!example] Examples
> - In "Goku defeated Vegeta," Goku is the subject.
> - The subject of the sentence is the warrior.
> - Identify the subject and the predicate.

### Sense 4 — *Noun* (Common)
A person under the rule of a monarch or government; a citizen.

> [!example] Examples
> - The subjects of King Kai include numerous warriors.
> - All the subjects must obey the law.
> - He was a loyal subject of the kingdom.

### Sense 5 — *Verb* (Common)
To cause someone or something to experience something, usually unwelcome.

> [!example] Examples
> - Don't subject me to such intense training!
> - The fighters were subjected to rigorous testing.
> - He subjected them to his merciless attacks.

## 📜 Etymology
From Latin "subjectus" meaning "thrown under" or "placed beneath," from "sub-" (under) + "jacere" (to throw). The sense evolved from physical placement to grammatical function to topics of discussion.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Highly versatile word with multiple meanings
> - Noun uses: topic, school course, grammar role, citizen
> - Verb meaning: to cause to experience something (often unpleasant)
> - "Subject to" can mean "conditional upon" or "liable to be affected by"
> - Very common in both casual and academic contexts
> - "Sensitive subject" indicates a topic that might cause offense
