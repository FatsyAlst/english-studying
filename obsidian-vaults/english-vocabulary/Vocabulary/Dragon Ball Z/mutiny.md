## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *Noun* (Common)
An open rebellion or refusal to obey orders by members of the armed forces (especially soldiers or sailors against their commanders); a rebellion against authority.

> [!example] Examples
> - A mutiny broke out on the ship.
> - The mutiny was led by senior officers.
> - The crew staged a mutiny against the harsh captain.

### Sense 2 — *Verb* (Common)
To rise up in rebellion or to refuse to obey orders; to participate in a mutiny.

> [!example] Examples
> - The soldiers mutinied against their commanding officer.
> - The crew mutinied when conditions became unbearable.

## 📜 Etymology
From Italian *ammutinamento*, from *ammutinare* (to mutiny), related to French *mutinerie*. Possibly from *mute* (mute, silent), as in "rising up without speaking orders."

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Historically associated with naval and military contexts
> - Implies organized, collective rebellion with some seriousness
> - Distinct from simple insubordination (a single act of disobedience)
> - Carries dramatic and serious connotations
> - Synonyms: rebellion, uprising, revolt, insurrection, refusal to obey
> - Often used in historical, military, or adventure narratives
> - Can be used metaphorically for non-military contexts
