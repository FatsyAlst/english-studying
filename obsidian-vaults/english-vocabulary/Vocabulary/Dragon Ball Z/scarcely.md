## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adverb* (common)
Barely; almost not; only just; with difficulty.

> [!example] Examples
> - He could scarcely believe his eyes at the warrior's power.
> - She had scarcely begun her attack when it was over.
> - They scarcely had time to escape before the explosion.

### Sense 2 — *adverb* (common)
Definitely not; probably not; hardly likely.

> [!example] Examples
> - That excuse is scarcely believable given the evidence.
> - He scarcely deserves such respect for his cowardly actions.
> - This outcome was scarcely surprising after his poor performance.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French "escarce" (stingy, sparse), derived from Latin "excerpere" (to pluck out). The suffix "-ly" converts the adjective "scarce" to an adverb. Entered English in the 13th century.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - More formal than "barely" or "hardly"
> - Often used with inversion in formal writing: "Scarcely had he begun..."
> - Common in dramatic or literary contexts
> - Implies both physical scarcity and temporal narrowness
