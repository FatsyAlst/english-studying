## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Growing thickly and healthily with a lot of green plants or leaves; luxuriant and fertile.

> [!example] Examples
> - The lush green countryside was perfect for hiking.
> - Lush vegetation covered the tropical island.
> - The garden was lush with flowers and grass.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
Beautiful, rich, or luxurious in a way that gives pleasure (colors, sounds, furnishings, textures).

> [!example] Examples
> - The room had lush carpets and velvet curtains.
> - The lush tones of the orchestra filled the hall.
> - The painting featured lush colors and intricate details.

### Sense 3 — *adjective* (Common) — _UK informal_
Very attractive or pleasant, especially of a person.

> [!example] Examples
> - He's so lush in that photo!
> - She looks absolutely lush today.

### Sense 4 — *noun* (Common) — _slang, derogatory_
A person who drinks too much alcohol; a drunkard or alcoholic.

> [!example] Examples
> - He's just an old lush who drinks every day.
> - Don't become a lush like your father.

### Sense 5 — *noun* (Less Common) — _slang_
Alcohol or intoxicating liquor; booze.

> [!example] Examples
> - Let's get some lush at the pub.
> - He spends all his money on lush.

---
Late Middle English: alteration of obsolete *lash* 'soft, lax' from Old French *lasche*, associated with *luscious*. Noun 'drunkard' from 1890s slang (perhaps from 'lush' as liquor 1790s). The slang 'attractive' sense (UK, youth culture) emerged in late 20th century.

---
> [!tip] Notes
> - Adjective is positive for nature/luxury; noun is derogatory for drunk.
> - UK slang 'attractive' is casual/youthful, not formal.
> - The noun 'lush' is old-fashioned slang; 'alcoholic' is more modern/respectful.
> - Adjective meanings (luxuriant, luxurious) are the most common in modern usage.
