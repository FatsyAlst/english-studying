## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
Wide; large from one side to the other.

> [!example] Examples
> - He has broad shoulders.
> - a broad avenue with trees on both sides
> - The river is about fifty metres broad here.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
General rather than detailed; including many different things.

> [!example] Examples
> - In broad terms, the project was a success.
> - The course covers a broad range of topics.
> - There is broad agreement on the main issues.

### Sense 3 — *adjective* (Common)
(Of an accent) very strong and noticeable.

> [!example] Examples
> - He speaks with a broad Scottish accent.

### Sense 4 — *adjective, in "in broad daylight"* (Common)
*In broad daylight* = during the day, when everything can be seen clearly (often about crimes).

> [!example] Examples
> - My bike was stolen in broad daylight.

### Sense 5 — *noun* (Rare — Slang, AmE, dated and offensive)
A woman (old gangster-film slang).

> [!example] Examples
> - "She's one tough broad," said the detective in the old movie.

## 📜 Etymology
Old English *brād* "wide". The slang noun "woman" is recorded in American English from 1911; its origin is uncertain.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - *Broad* vs *wide*: *wide* is more common for physical distance (*a wide road*); *broad* is typical for body parts (*broad shoulders*) and range (*a broad range*).
> - *Broadly speaking* = in general.
> - The slang noun is old-fashioned and offensive today — recognise it, don't use it.
