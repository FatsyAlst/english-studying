## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb, "try to do"* (Most Common)
To attempt to do something, especially something difficult.

> [!example] Examples
> - I tried to call you, but my phone died.
> - Try not to be late tomorrow.
> - We've been trying to fix this bug all day.

### Sense 2 — *verb, "try doing"* (Most Common)
To do something as an experiment, to see if it works or if you like it.

> [!example] Examples
> - If your laptop freezes, try restarting it.
> - Have you tried playing Juno? She's fun.
> - Try adding a bit more salt.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
To test or sample something (food, clothes, a new thing); *try on* (clothes), *try out* (something new).

> [!example] Examples
> - You should try the waffles in Leuven.
> - Can I try on these shoes?
> - Let's try out the new hero in quick play.

### Sense 4 — *verb* (Common — Legal)
To examine a person or case in a court of law.

> [!example] Examples
> - He was tried for fraud and found guilty.

### Sense 5 — *verb* (Less Common)
To test the limits of someone's patience.

> [!example] Examples
> - My neighbour's music at 2 a.m. is really trying my patience.

### Sense 6 — *noun* (Common)
An attempt.

> [!example] Examples
> - Give it a try!
> - I passed on my second try.
> - Nice try!

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *trier* "to sift, pick out"; the senses "attempt" and "examine in court" both developed in Middle English (14th century).

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - KEY GRAMMAR: *try **to** do* = make an effort (it's difficult) vs *try do**ing*** = experiment (see if it helps).
> - *Trying* (adjective) = difficult or annoying: *a trying day*.
> - *Try and do* is informal (mainly BrE speech) for *try to do*.
> - Related: *give it a whirl* — see [[whirl]].
