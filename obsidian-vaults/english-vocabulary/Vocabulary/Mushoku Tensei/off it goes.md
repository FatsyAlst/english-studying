## 📖 Definition
*expression* (Common — Informal)
Used to say, in a lively way, that something leaves or starts moving or happening. Part of the pattern *off* + pronoun + *go*: *Off we go! / Off you go! / Off they went.*

> [!example] Examples
> - I press the button and off it goes — the 3D printer starts printing.
> - She put the letter in the envelope, and off it went.
> - Right, everyone's here — off we go!

## 🔗 Collocations
- off we go!
- off you go!
- and off it went
- off he goes again (= he's starting his usual behaviour again)

---
## 📜 Etymology
Fronting an adverb (*off* before the subject) is an old pattern used to make narration more vivid, like *Away they ran* or *Here comes the bus*.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - With a pronoun the order is **off + pronoun + verb**: *Off it goes* (not *Off goes it*).
> - With a noun subject, inversion is common: *Off goes the train!*
> - *Off you go!* = an instruction or encouragement to leave or start.
> - *Off he goes again* = an annoyed comment about someone repeating a habit.
> - Grammar link: present simple for live narration and commentary.
