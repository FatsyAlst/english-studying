## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Most Common)
To officially introduce a rule, tax, punishment, limit etc. and force people to accept it.

> [!example] Examples
> - The government imposed a new tax on plastic.
> - The professor imposed a strict word limit on the essay.
> - The EU imposed sanctions on the country.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To force someone to accept your opinions, beliefs or presence.

> [!example] Examples
> - Don't impose your opinions on others.
> - My parents never imposed their religion on me.

### Sense 3 — *verb* (Common)
*Impose (on someone)* = to ask or expect too much from someone; to cause them trouble.

> [!example] Examples
> - I don't want to impose, but could I stay at your place tonight?
> - Are you sure? I don't want to impose on you.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From French *imposer*, adapting Latin *impōnere* "to place upon" (*in-* "upon" + *pōnere* "to put").

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Pattern: impose something **on** someone/something.
> - *I don't want to impose* = a polite phrase when someone offers hospitality.
> - *Imposing* (adjective) = impressive in size or appearance.
> - Portuguese cognate: *impor*.
