## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *adjective* (Most Common)
*Be fond of* = to like someone or something very much, especially something you have known for a long time.

> [!example] Examples
> - I'm very fond of my grandmother.
> - She's fond of old Dragon Ball episodes.
> - He's not very fond of cold weather.

### Sense 2 — *adjective* (Common)
(Before a noun) affectionate, loving; (of memories) happy.

> [!example] Examples
> - I have fond memories of my time in São Carlos.
> - She gave him a fond smile.

### Sense 3 — *adjective* (Less Common — Formal)
(Of a hope or belief) unlikely to come true; foolishly hopeful.

> [!example] Examples
> - It was a fond hope that the trains would be on time.

---
## 📜 Etymology
Middle English *fonned* "foolish", from *fon* "fool". The meaning moved from "foolish" to "foolishly affectionate" and finally to simply "affectionate".

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - *Be fond of* is gentler and warmer than *love*; often long-term affection.
> - *Grow fond of* = gradually start to like.
> - *Fond memories* is an extremely common collocation.
> - A state adjective — *I'm being fond of* ✗.
