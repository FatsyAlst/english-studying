## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A vegetable, especially a cucumber, preserved in vinegar or salt water; (BrE) a thick, spicy sauce made of preserved vegetables.

> [!example] Examples
> - I always ask for extra pickles on my burger.
> - Do you want a pickle with your sandwich?
> - A cheese and pickle sandwich is a British classic.

### Sense 2 — *verb* (Common)
To preserve food in vinegar or salt water.

> [!example] Examples
> - My grandmother used to pickle onions and peppers.
> - Pickling food was essential before fridges existed.
> - These cucumbers have been pickled for two weeks.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Common — Informal)
A difficult or unpleasant situation, in the idiom *in a pickle*.

> [!example] Examples
> - I left my keys inside the flat — I'm in a bit of a pickle.
> - We'll be in a real pickle if the train is cancelled.
> - Thanks for getting me out of that pickle.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From Middle Dutch / Middle Low German *pekel* "brine" (15th century). The "difficult situation" sense is from the 16th century — Shakespeare uses "in this pickle" in *The Tempest*.

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - In AmE *a pickle* usually means a pickled cucumber (BrE: *gherkin*).
> - *Pickled* can informally mean drunk.
> - *Pickling* (gerund) = the process of preserving food.
> - Portuguese: *picles* (cognate).
