## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *verb* (Common)
To have a very strong, unpleasant smell.

> [!example] Examples
> - His clothes reeked of booze after the party.
> - The whole flat reeks of cigarette smoke.
> - The fridge reeks — something has gone off.

### Sense 2 — *verb (figurative)* (Common)
To strongly suggest something bad or suspicious.

> [!example] Examples
> - The whole deal reeks of corruption.
> - This plan reeks of desperation.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Less Common)
A strong, unpleasant smell.

> [!example] Examples
> - The reek of rotten fish filled the market.

---
## 📜 Etymology
From Old English *rēocan* "to give off smoke or vapour". In Scots *reek* still means smoke — Edinburgh's old nickname is *Auld Reekie* ("Old Smoky").

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - Followed by *of* when you name the smell: *reek of booze*.
> - Stronger and more negative than *smell*; similar to *stink*.
> - The figurative use (*reeks of corruption*) is common in journalism.
> - Seen in Dr. Stone as *reeked of booze* — see [[booze]].
