## 📖 Definition
*idiom* (Common — Informal)
To work extremely hard, usually for a long time.

> [!example] Examples
> - I've been working my tail off all week to finish the lab report.
> - She worked her tail off to get into engineering school.
> - They're working their tails off in the kitchen tonight.

---
## 🌳 Word Family
| Part of Speech | Forms |
|----------------|-------|
| Idiom | work one's tail off (work my / your / her tail off…) |
| Variants | work one's butt off (informal), work one's socks off (BrE), work one's fingers to the bone |

---
## 🔗 Collocations
- work my tail off
- have been working our tails off
- work your tail off for (something)
- work their tails off to (do something)

---
## 📜 Etymology
Mid-20th-century American English. *Tail* is an informal word for the backside, as in the stronger variants *work one's butt / ass off*; the image is of working so hard that part of you "falls off".

---
## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - The possessive changes with the subject: I work **my** tail off, she works **her** tail off.
> - Not the phrasal verb *tail off* (= to become gradually smaller or quieter: *His voice tailed off*).
> - Very natural with the present perfect continuous: *I've been working my tail off.*
> - ⚠️ Similar meaning in [[bust one's back]] (from [[Dr. Stone]])
