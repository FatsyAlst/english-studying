## 📖 Definition
### Sense 1 — *noun* (Most Common)
A mixture of cement, sand and water used to join bricks or stones in building.

> [!example] Examples
> - The bricks are held together with mortar.
> - The mortar between the old stones was crumbling.
> - Mix the mortar to the right consistency before laying the bricks.

### Sense 2 — *noun* (Common)
A short, wide cannon that fires shells high into the air.

> [!example] Examples
> - The town came under mortar fire.
> - The soldiers set up a mortar on the hill.

### Sense 3 — *noun* (Less Common)
A hard bowl in which substances are crushed with a *pestle*.

> [!example] Examples
> - Grind the spices with a mortar and pestle.
> - In the lab, we crushed the crystals in a mortar.

## 📜 Etymology
From Old French *mortier*, from Latin *mortārium* "bowl for pounding; the mixture prepared in it". The cannon was named after its short, wide, bowl-like shape.

## 💡 Usage Notes
> [!tip] Notes
> - *Bricks-and-mortar* business = a traditional physical shop (vs online).
> - *Mortarboard* = the flat square cap worn at graduation.
> - Portuguese: *argamassa* (building), *morteiro* (cannon), *almofariz / pilão* (bowl).
> - Civil engineering and lab vocabulary.
