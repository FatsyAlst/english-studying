## Climate vs Weather

Both words talk about rain, sun, wind and temperature, so they feel interchangeable. They aren't: the difference is **time**.

---

### Weather

**Weather** is the state of the atmosphere at a particular place and time — *today*, *this afternoon*, *this week*. It changes all the time.

1. **Short-term conditions** — *"The weather in Leuven is grey and rainy today."*
2. **Uncountable noun** — no *a*, no plural: *"What's the weather like?"* (✗ *a weather*, ✗ *weathers*)
3. **Idioms** — *under the weather* (= slightly ill), *weather the storm* (= survive a difficult period)

> [!example] Typical usage
> - *What's the weather like in São Paulo right now?*
> - *The weather forecast says it'll snow on Friday.*
> - *I'm a bit under the weather, so I'm skipping the lab.*

---

### Climate

**Climate** is the *average* weather of a region over a long period — meteorologists use periods of **30 years**. It describes what is typical, not what is happening now.

1. **Long-term patterns** — *"Belgium has a mild, wet maritime climate."*
2. **Countable noun** — *"a tropical climate"*, *"different climates"*
3. **Figurative** — the general mood or situation: *"the political climate"*, *"the economic climate"*

> [!example] Typical usage
> - *Brazil has a tropical climate in most regions.*
> - *Climate change is making heatwaves more frequent.*
> - *In the current economic climate, internships are hard to find.*

---

### Key Differences

| Aspect | Weather | Climate |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Time scale** | Hours, days, weeks | Decades (30-year averages) |
| **Grammar** | Uncountable (*the weather*) | Countable (*a / the climate*, *climates*) |
| **Question** | *What's the weather like today?* | *What's the climate like in Belgium?* |
| **Science** | Meteorology, forecasts | Climatology, climate models |
| **Figurative use** | *under the weather*, *weather the storm* | *the political / business climate* |

---

### The Portuguese Trap

In everyday Brazilian Portuguese, *clima* is used for both ideas: *"O clima hoje está quente"*. In English that sentence must use **weather**:

- ✅ *The weather is hot today.*
- ❌ *The climate is hot today.*

Use **climate** only when you talk about what a place is like *in general*, year after year. (*Tempo* is the closer translation of *weather*: *"Como está o tempo?"* = *"What's the weather like?"*)

> [!tip] The Shortcut
> A popular saying sums it up: **"Climate is what you expect; weather is what you get."**
> If the sentence has *today*, *tomorrow* or *this week*, you almost certainly need **weather**.

Bonus homophones: **weather** (tempo) / **whether** (se) / **wether** (a castrated ram — you will probably never need this one).
