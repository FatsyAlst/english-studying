## Who vs Whom

This is the distinction that trips up native speakers and learners alike. Most people just use "who" for everything and hope for the best. But once you see the logic, it's actually straightforward.

---

### The Core Rule

**Who** = subject (the one *doing* the action)
**Whom** = object (the one *receiving* the action)

That's it. The entire rule fits in two lines.

> [!example] The test in action
> - ***Who** called you?* → "He called me." (subject — ✅ **who**)
> - ***Whom** did you call?* → "I called him." (object — ✅ **whom**)

---

### The "Him/He" Trick

The fastest way to know which one to use: try answering the question with **he** or **him**.

- If the answer is **he/she/they** → use **who**
- If the answer is **him/her/them** → use **whom**

Both "whom" and "him" end in **m**. That's your mnemonic.

| Question | Test answer | Correct form |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| *\___ is at the door?* | *He* is at the door | **Who** |
| *\___ did you invite?* | I invited *him* | **Whom** |
| *\___ wrote this?* | *She* wrote this | **Who** |
| *To \___ should I send it?* | Send it to *her* | **Whom** |
| *\___ are you talking about?* | I'm talking about *them* | **Whom** |

---

### With Prepositions

**Whom** almost always follows prepositions: *to whom, for whom, with whom, by whom, from whom*.

- *The person **to whom** I spoke was very helpful.*
- *A teacher **for whom** we have great respect.*
- ***By whom** was this painting created?*

In casual speech, people often move the preposition to the end and drop "whom" entirely: *"Who did you speak to?"* — this is perfectly normal and accepted in everyday English.

> [!tip] Formality scale
> - **Formal:** *To whom did you address the letter?*
> - **Neutral:** *Who did you address the letter to?*
> - **Casual:** *Who'd you send that to?*

---

### Is "Whom" Dying?

In everyday spoken English, yes — "whom" is increasingly rare. Most native speakers use "who" in all positions and no one blinks. You'll sound perfectly natural saying *"Who did you call?"* instead of *"Whom did you call?"*

But **whom** is alive and well in:

- **Formal writing** (academic papers, business correspondence, legal documents)
- **Fixed expressions** ("to whom it may concern")
- **Literary and archaic contexts** (where it pairs nicely with words like [[whence]] and [[thee]])
- **After prepositions** in formal speech ("for whom the bell tolls")

---

## Bonus: Whose

Since we're here, let's complete the trio. **Whose** is the possessive form — it asks about ownership or belonging.

**Whose** = *belonging to whom*

> [!example] Whose in action
> - ***Whose** jacket is this?* → It's *his* jacket.
> - ***Whose** idea was that?* → It was *her* idea.
> - *The author **whose** book I read just won an award.*

A common trap: **whose vs who's**.

- **Whose** = possessive (*Whose pen is this?*)
- **Who's** = contraction of "who is" or "who has" (*Who's coming tonight?*)

If you can expand it to "who is," use **who's**. Otherwise, **whose**.

| Word | Function | Replaces | Example |
|------|----------|----------|---------|
| **Who** | Subject | he, she, they | *Who said that?* |
| **Whom** | Object | him, her, them | *Whom did you ask?* |
| **Whose** | Possessive | his, her, their | *Whose turn is it?* |
| **Who's** | Contraction | who is / who has | *Who's ready?* |

---

### Quick-Fire Practice

Try these mentally before reading the answers:

1. *\___ is responsible for this mess?*
2. *The student \___ essay won first place...*
3. *\___ are you going with?*
4. *\___ been eating my food?*

> [!tip]- Answers
> 1. **Who** (he is responsible → subject)
> 2. **Whose** (his essay → possessive)
> 3. **Whom** (going with him → object of preposition)
> 4. **Who's** (who has been eating → contraction)
